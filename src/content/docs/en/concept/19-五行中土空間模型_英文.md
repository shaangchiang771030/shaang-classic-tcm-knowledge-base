When the Five Phases move from the cyclical, ring-like structure of the **time axis** into **spatial geometric distribution**, their topology changes fundamentally. This gives rise to the **Five-Phase spatial model with Earth at the center**.
In space, the Five Phases are not arranged as a simple circle in linear succession. Instead, **Earth** occupies the central geometric origin and governs an orthogonal framework extending toward east, west, south, and north.

In this spatial Five-Phase model, Wood occupies the east, Fire the south, Metal the west, Water the north, while **Earth resides in the center**. Earth is the pivot through which the energies of the four directions converge and transform, stabilizing the entire geometric system. This is orthogonal to the temporal Five-Phase model, in which Earth participates in the cycle as long summer between summer and autumn.

The model receives a particularly clear classical presentation in the “Discourse on the True Words of the Golden Chamber” in the *Huangdi Neijing*. In the geometric organization of that text, the phenomena derived from the five directions—east, south, west, north, and center—such as the five winds, five zang, and five tastes, are attached to a model centered on Earth. The spatial key to this model is the first-principles idea behind the zang-xiang statement that **“the Spleen is the solitary zang; central Earth irrigates the four sides.”**
