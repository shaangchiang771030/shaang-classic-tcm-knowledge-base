Besides short cycles such as respiration and the nutritive–defensive rhythms, the ancients also described long life cycles centered on Kidney qi and *tiangui*. Women are described in seven-year stages and men in eight-year stages. Kidney qi grows, reaches an even maturity, and then gradually declines; *tiangui* arrives and later becomes exhausted; the Ren vessel, Great Chong vessel, reproductive capacity, sinews and bones, hair, teeth, and overall bodily form change in sequence.

The value of seven and eight is therefore not that they are mysterious numbers. What is worth observing is that the ancients attempted to describe growth, reproductive activation, peak development, and aging through a series of major physiological turning points.

In the article “The Beginning of Human Life and the Formation of Ancestral Qi,” the ten months of gestation answer the question: **how is the life network sequentially established and nourished?** The seven-/eight-year rhythm answers a different question: **how does that same network mature, activate reproduction, reach a peak, and then decline by subsystem?**

Across the prenatal/postnatal divide, the classics repeatedly use easily observed structures—teeth, sinews, bones, and hair—as markers. Precisely because they are visible and can now also be evaluated with modern imaging, they can serve as concrete physiological indicators for otherwise invisible changes in the classical model of qi.

**Growth phase:** Kidney qi becomes abundant → *tiangui* arrives → Ren and Chong become freely connected and full → sinews, bones, and bodily form flourish.

## Peak phase

**Decline phase:** functions associated with Yangming, the Three Yang, Kidney, Liver, Ren/Chong and other systems gradually decline → *tiangui* is exhausted.

When this chapter is read only as a theory of aging, another aspect is easily missed: the classical descriptions also imply directions for regulation. For example, the sequence of hair whitening, hair loss, tooth change, and channel decline suggests which functional systems the tradition would examine when considering problems such as prematurely white hair.

## Original Passage from *Exploring the Sources of Medicine*

“At seven years in a girl, Kidney qi is abundant, the teeth change and the hair grows. At two sevens, *tiangui* arrives, the Ren vessel becomes open and the Great Chong vessel flourishes; menstruation descends according to its time, therefore she can conceive. At three sevens, Kidney qi is even, the wisdom teeth emerge and growth reaches its full extent. At four sevens, the sinews and bones are firm, the hair reaches full length, and the body is strong. At five sevens, the Yangming vessel declines, the hair begins to fall and the face begins to wither. At six sevens, the Three Yang vessels decline above, the face is withered and the hair begins to whiten. At seven sevens, the Ren vessel is deficient, the Great Chong declines and becomes scant, *tiangui* is exhausted, the way of Earth is no longer open; bodily form deteriorates and she can no longer conceive.

“At eight years in a boy, Kidney qi is substantial, hair grows and teeth change. At two eights, Kidney qi flourishes, *tiangui* arrives, Essence-Qi overflows and discharges; Yin and Yang harmonize, therefore he can father children. At three eights, Kidney qi is even, sinews and bones are strong, the wisdom teeth emerge and growth reaches its full extent. At four eights, sinews and bones are at their peak and the muscles are full and strong. At five eights, Kidney qi declines, the hair falls and teeth dry. At six eights, Yang qi declines above, the face withers and the temples turn gray-white. At seven eights, Liver qi declines, the sinews can no longer move freely, *tiangui* is exhausted, Essence becomes scant, the Kidney zang declines, and bodily form reaches its limit. At eight eights, hair and teeth are lost.”
