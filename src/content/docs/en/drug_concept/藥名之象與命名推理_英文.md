We emphasize that ancient naming was not arbitrary labeling. Many drug names themselves preserve clues to the medicine’s **nature, flavor, form, color, timing, direction, and function**. In decoding the *Shennong Bencao Jing*, the first step is often not to look up “effects,” but to understand the name.

  <figcaption>Drug names, form, color, and image often constitute the first layer of reasoning left by the ancients, helping us return from a name to the substance’s innate endowment.</figcaption>
</figure>

Naming can be divided into several types: names based on color, such as *Baizhi* and red adzuki bean; names based on flavor, such as licorice; names based on form, such as *Wutou*; names based on season, such as coltsfoot flower and *Xiakucao*; and names based on the direction or function of qi, which must be analyzed within the symptomatology and qi-transformation models of the *Inner Canon*. Together these names form an entrance into the ancients’ image-based mode of thought.

A figurative name such as *Wutou* immediately evokes associations with bodily location and force. *Xiakucao* joins the three characters “summer,” “wither,” and “grass,” suggesting a distinctive endowment within seasonal timing and the Four Images. Memorizing effects alone misses the reasoning path contained in the name itself.
