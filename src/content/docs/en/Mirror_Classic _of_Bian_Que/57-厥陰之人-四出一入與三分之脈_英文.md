Compared with the preceding two types, the breath-pulse structure of the Jueyin-type person is even more distinctive:

> Four out, one in: this is the Jueyin-type person, whose pulse lü are Yingzhong and Wushe. The Jueyin pulse oscillates three fen and moves through the divided flesh; with inhalation long and exhalation repeated, the pulse moves six times, while qi travels six cun—this is normal.

Here, six movements in one breath can still be “normal.”

## Six movements cannot by themselves equal a rapid pathological pulse

If one uses a single universal pulse count, six movements can easily be classified directly as fast. But the *Mirror Classic* requires simultaneous consideration of four-out-one-in, long inhalation and repeated exhalation, three-fen oscillation, and the Yingzhong-Wushe lü pair.

The number six has meaning only within the complete structure.

## Why is Jueyin three fen?

“Qiheng” already stated: “the pulses of Yangming and Jueyin surge three fen and rise like a ridge.” The individual-type chapter agrees with that baseline.

The source also describes the normal shape at three fen as “floating in spring, like a fish swimming in waves,” bringing seasonal background and pulse morphology into the comparison.

## Hand and Foot Jueyin are separated by leaping and non-leaping

The text says that when Nourishing qi is abundant and Defense strong, with oscillation and leaping, this is Foot Jueyin; when essence-qi rises strongly and moves without leaping, this is Hand Jueyin.

Even within the same type, waveform direction and leaping further differentiate hand and foot channels.

## Why does this section suddenly discuss the Bao-network at such length?

The source then gives a long description of the Hand Jueyin Bao-network arising from the Bao center, connecting with the testicular system, linking the Heart system, and passing through Sanjiao, and says:

> The Bao-network is the root of continuation and reproduction, the ancestral beginning of original qi.

It further distinguishes the male Bao storing essence from the female Bao storing blood.

Within the *Mirror Classic*’s model, Jueyin therefore has a particularly strong relation to the Bao-network, original qi, and the roots of reproduction rather than representing only a single “Liver channel” concept.
