### Yin-Yang and the Five Phases Are Not Fixed Diagrams, but a Modeling Language for Time and Space

Yin-yang, the Four Images, Five Phases, Heavenly Stems and Earthly Branches, the Three Yin and Three Yang, the twenty-four solar terms, and the Five Movements and Six Qi are among the concepts in classical Chinese medicine that modern readers most easily experience as “mystical.” The main difficulty is not simply the number of terms. It is that models designed for different purposes and scales are often collapsed onto one diagram: once we see “Wood,” we assume every use of Wood refers to the same thing; once we see Shaoyang, we assume Shaoyang at an annual scale, a daily scale, and in human spatial organization must denote one fixed entity.

The Spacetime domain addresses that problem. Its goal is not to restore one universal yin-yang/Five-Phase diagram, but to recover a deeper modeling method: how the ancients compressed periodic natural phenomena into a small number of symbols, how different temporal and spatial scales could be modeled separately, and how those models could later be combined.

The proper starting point is therefore methodology rather than the Five Phases. [Extracting First Principles from the Classics](/en/concept/01-第一性原理的經典萃取/) asks whether the dense descriptions of the classics can be reduced to a smaller set of irreducible rules. [The Modeling Principle of Governing Complexity with Simplicity](/en/concept/02-以簡馭繁的模型化原則/) explains why a model is valuable precisely when fewer rules can account for more phenomena. [The Validity of Occam's Razor](/en/concept/06-奧卡姆剃刀法則的有效性/) must follow immediately, because a model that saves every counterexample by adding another exception may appear able to “explain everything” while losing predictive force. [Inferring the Interior from the Exterior and Testing the Invisible](/en/concept/09-以外揣內與不可見本質的驗證/) adds another discipline: what cannot be observed directly must be inferred from observable consequences and remain open to falsification. [Correct Calendars and the Law of Temporal Scales](/en/concept/10-正確歷法的時間刻度規律/) then reminds us that any cyclic model is meaningless if its starting point, endpoint, and temporal scale are not correctly aligned.

Only after these guardrails are in place should we read [The Principle of Symbolic Energy Resonance](/en/concept/03-符號化能量共振原則/), [Temporal Holography and the Mapping of the World](/en/concept/04-全息規律的世界映射/), and [The Law of Spatial Holographic Mapping](/en/concept/05-空間全息映射規律/). These are among the easiest articles to misread. They are best treated as hypotheses about symbolic modeling and cross-scale analogy, not as modern physics retroactively proving the ancient texts. [Fitting the Principle of Constancy and Change](/en/concept/08-常變原則的擬合規律/) then provides a highly practical distinction between a relatively stable baseline and variables that change over time, while [The Principle of Cross-Scale Model Integration](/en/concept/07-跨尺度技術搭配原則/) prepares the ground for combining models across scales. At this point the reader has a method for using symbols without being led by them.

The next step is the simplest binary model. [Annual Yin-Yang Time Model](/en/concept/11-年尺度陰陽時間模型/) uses the year to model the waxing and waning of yin and yang, while [Daily Yin-Yang Time Model](/en/concept/12-日尺度陰陽時間模型/) compresses the same language into the scale of a day. [Human Yin-Yang Spatial Geometric Symmetry Model](/en/concept/13-人體陰陽空間幾何對稱模型/) and [Heaven-Earth Yin-Yang Spatial Model](/en/concept/14-天地的陰陽空間模型/) then deliberately change the axis from time to space. This is the lesson: the same terms “yin” and “yang” can describe temporal increase-decrease or spatial interior-exterior, upper-lower, and anterior-posterior relations. The symbols may be the same while the coordinate axes are not.

A binary system alone cannot represent transition, so [Four Images Time Model: The Law of Mutual Inclusion of Yin and Yang](/en/concept/15-四象時間模型陰陽互含規律/) introduces yang within yin and yin within yang, allowing the cycle to contain intermediate states rather than only two extremes. [Four Images Model of Lunar Phases](/en/concept/16-月相四象變化模型/) tests that structure on another cycle—the lunar phases. The point is not merely to discuss the Moon, but to train the reader to ask whether a model truly transfers across cycles and what evidence a cross-scale analogy requires.

Only then do we enter the Five Phases. [Annual Five-Phase Generating Cycle Model](/en/concept/17-五行相生年尺度模型/) first treats Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, and Water as stages in an annual process of generation, growth, transformation, gathering, and storage. [Ten-Day Five-Phase Cycle of the Heavenly Stems](/en/concept/18-天干十日五行時間週期模型/) moves the Five Phases into the finer temporal cycle of the Heavenly Stems. [Five-Phase Spatial Model with Earth at the Center](/en/concept/19-五行中土空間模型/) deliberately changes to a spatial architecture with Earth at the center; [Luoshu Single-Stroke Model of Five-Phase Restraint](/en/concept/20-五行相剋洛書一筆畫模型/) examines the different topology created by mutual restraint; and [Five-Phase Mutual-Inclusion Model: Refining Constitution and Medicinal Essence](/en/concept/21-五行互涵模型體質與藥精細分/) asks what happens when the Five Phases are further subdivided for higher-resolution classification. By this point, one principle should be firmly established: **the Five Phases are not one diagram but a family of models.** Generation, restraint, central Earth, temporal cycles, spatial organization, and mutual inclusion answer different questions.

The next model family is the Three Yin and Three Yang. [Annual Host-Qi Model of the Three Yin and Three Yang](/en/concept/23-三陰三陽年尺度主氣模型/) should come first because host qi provides a relatively fixed six-step climatic baseline. [Partitioning Model of the Twenty-Four Solar Terms](/en/concept/27-二十四節氣拆分模型/) increases the temporal resolution of that annual axis. Only after the host baseline is clear should [Three Yin and Three Yang Guest-Qi Model Across Time Scales](/en/concept/22-三陰三陽年尺度客氣變化模型/) introduce guest qi as a variable yearly perturbation. This “host first, guest second” order is the practical application of the earlier distinction between constancy and change.

[Twelve-Day Earthly-Branch Model of the Three Yin and Three Yang](/en/concept/24-三陰三陽地支十二日時間模型/) then compresses the Three Yin and Three Yang into a twelve-day Earthly-Branch sequence; [Daily Desired-Relief-Time Model of the Three Yin and Three Yang](/en/concept/25-三陰三陽日尺度預解時模型/) brings the model into different hours within a day; [Human Spatial Distribution of the Three Yin and Three Yang: The Law of Opening, Closing, and Pivoting](/en/concept/26-三陰三陽人體空間分佈模型陰陽離合規律/) shifts again from time to the spatial organization of the human body. As with yin-yang and the Five Phases, the repeated lesson is not to force identical symbols at different scales into a single fixed object. The Three Yin and Three Yang provide a reusable grammar of partition and movement whose concrete axis depends on the question being asked.

[Nine Palaces and Eight Winds: Spatial and Diagnostic Model](/en/concept/29-九宮八風空間與診法模型/) is best kept relatively independent. It uses a different three-by-three spatial matrix for understanding the Nine Fields, Three Divisions and Nine Indicators, and positional differentiation. Precisely because this topology is not identical to Five-Phase or Six-Qi topology, preserving its independence protects against the tendency to equate every nine-palace structure with the Five Phases.

Only now is [Five-Six Spatiotemporal Integration Model](/en/concept/28-五六相合時空整合模型/) ready to combine the Five Phases and the Three Yin and Three Yang. The models can be multiplied only after their definitions, scales, and coordinates are separately clear; otherwise “Five-Six integration” becomes little more than a pile of symbols. [Principle of Energy Superposition and Intervention](/en/concept/30-能量運動的疊加與干預原則/) then brings the domain to its real endpoint: a real spacetime point is rarely governed by a single cycle. Annual, monthly, daily, spatial, individual, and environmental conditions may overlap at once. The question is no longer simply “What phase is this year?” but which independent influences are superposed here, which are background conditions, and which are becoming the dominant disturbance.

The central competence built by this domain is therefore **model recognition**. When you see yin-yang, ask which scale and axis are being described. When you see the Five Phases, ask whether the problem concerns time, space, generation, or restraint. When you see the Three Yin and Three Yang, distinguish host qi, guest qi, day sequence, desired-relief time, and human spatial distribution. Keep constants and variables separate, and combine models only when their dimensions are explicit.

With that discipline, yin-yang and the Five Phases cease to be a classification trick that forces everything into five or six boxes. They begin to recover what the classical tradition used them for most powerfully: **describing recurring temporal cycles, spatial partitions, and multiscale interactions in nature and the human body with a very small symbolic vocabulary.**
