Within the larger family of models in the study of Heaven and humanity, there is, in addition to the doctrines of movement and qi that track temporal change, a special model concerned with the **spatial regularities of Earth**: the **Nine Palaces and Eight Winds spatial and diagnostic model**. It uses an orderly **3 × 3 matrix** to bind the geometry of terrestrial space, directions of airflow, and the distribution of the body's “nine regions and nine fields” into a highly abstract topological framework.

Its source is the **Luoshu**. The nine-palace grid is presented as a minimal and elegant matrix through which the ancients marked positions and calculated dynamic energetic changes on two- and three-dimensional planes. In body space, it cross-multiplies upper, middle, and lower with Heaven, Earth, and humanity to form nine topological regions. In the external environment, it corresponds to the energetic influences of the **Eight Winds** arriving from eight geometric directions. It is therefore used as a spatial diagnostic framework for capturing the influence of earthly qi.

In the classical texts, this model is linked directly to the ancient and authoritative **three regions and nine indicators** method of the *Huangdi Neijing: Suwen*, in which the head, hands, and feet are each divided according to Heaven, Earth, and humanity to form nine diagnostic observation zones. Contemporary Chinese physician Dong Qianyang, in *Circular Movement of the Nine Palaces*, further places anatomical regions and the medicinally used parts of herbs into the same Nine-Palace geometry. For example, flower medicines are classified as Fire, south, and the upper part of the grid because plants are especially likely to flower in summer, the Fire season; the blossom is therefore said to encapsulate nature's most outward-dispersing Fire-Heat quality. In this way, the Nine Palaces model is presented as a tool for guiding both materia-medica reasoning and spatial diagnosis.
