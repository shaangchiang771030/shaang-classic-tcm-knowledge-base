When studying the Five Movements and Six Qi, it is easy to ask only one question: “What is the controlling celestial qi this year?” or “Which annual movement is this year?” Yet if we return to the seven Yunqi chapters of the *Huangdi Neijing*, the ancient description was never that a single label determines an entire year. The annual movement provides a year-scale background; Sitian and Zaiquan form the upper and lower qi; within the six steps there are host qi and guest qi; and the Five Movements themselves also include host movement and guest movement. In other words, a person is actually situated within several qi-transformation fields operating simultaneously at different time scales.

We therefore did not reduce forty thousand medical cases to the question of “whether a certain disease was common in a certain year.” Instead, for each case we placed all seven fields present at disease onset—the annual movement, host movement, guest movement, Sitian, Zaiquan, host qi, and guest qi—into one model, then examined whether Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, and Water showed same-element resonance.

This approach is close to the *Neijing* ideas of “like qi seeking like qi” and of qi and form having differences in abundance and decline. One Fire signal by itself is not the key issue; what matters is whether Fire is gathering simultaneously from several different layers.

Among roughly **38,700 cases** for which all seven fields could be calculated completely, we divided resonance from low to high. The result was quite direct. Among low-resonance cases, the proportion in which *Neijing* Yunqi pathomechanism pathways matched the patient’s symptoms well was only about **3%**. With moderate resonance, it rose to about **40%**; among high-resonance cases, it reached about **54%**. If we instead ask whether each patient showed at least one highly concordant symptom, the corresponding values rose from about **5% → 48% → 61%**.

The most interesting point, however, is not simply that “higher resonance means more severe disease.”

For example, when the seven fields form `5+1+1`, one Five-Phase element is highly concentrated, creating a strong “single-pole field.” A `4+3` structure has a slightly lower single peak, but contains two strong resonance clusters at the same time. The latter can open two different pathomechanism directions simultaneously, so the range of symptoms may actually be broader. This reminds us that what Chinese medicine calls “mixed” or “combined” patterns are not necessarily two disease names pasted together; two qi-transformation modes may be amplified at the same time.

The Five Movements and Six Qi are therefore best understood as a **probability field for pathomechanisms**, rather than as a diagnostic answer. They cannot explain every disease a person develops, nor should they be expected to. Constitution, diet, emotion, and pre-existing illness still matter. The climatic field merely raises the probability of certain pathomechanism pathways that were already possible.

This is precisely where the seven Yunqi chapters have their real value. They do not tell us, “You will definitely get a certain disease this year.” They describe something subtler: when multiple layers of Heaven-and-Earth qi resonate with one another, which pathomechanism pathways in the human body become easier to enter.
