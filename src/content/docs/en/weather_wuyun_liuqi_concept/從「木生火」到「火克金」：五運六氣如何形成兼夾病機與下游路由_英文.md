When all seven Yunqi fields act at the same time, one configuration deserves particular attention: `4+3`.

It means the seven fields are not dispersed. Instead, they gather into two strong Five-Phase clusters. For example, four fields may belong to Fire and three to Wood; or four may belong to Metal and three to Fire.

If we look only at “severity,” both are called 4+3. But Five-Phase theory in the *Neijing* reminds us that **Wood generates Fire, whereas Fire overcomes Metal. Equal strength does not imply the same pathomechanism.**

The actual calendrical structures in the forty thousand medical cases also demonstrate this. The 282 cases with a 4+3 configuration were not randomly distributed: about **248 cases were Fire4 + Wood3**, while about **32 cases were Metal4 + Fire3**. In other words, the real Five-Movements-and-Six-Qi calendar itself sharply constrains the state space that can actually occur.

In the “Wood3 generates Fire4” cases, symptoms such as cough, dyspnea, edema, vomiting, and vexing heat were more likely to cluster together. The pattern resembles a state in which Wood and Fire mutually reinforce one another and expand the active pathomechanism range at the same time. This is close to the Chinese-medicine idea of “Wind and Fire fanning one another”: it is not first an isolated Wind pattern and then an added Fire pattern, but two qi-transformation modes amplifying each other.

Even more noteworthy is “Fire3 overcomes Metal4.”

If pathomechanism stopped at a static Five-Phase diagram, we would expect mainly Fire- and Metal-related problems. But the actual Clinical Paths did not stop there. A large proportion routed onward toward **Water and Wood**. In the strongest subgroup, about ninety percent of classifiable pathomechanisms fell into these two downstream directions.

This begins to reveal something resembling a “cascade.” After one Five-Phase conflict occurs, the pathomechanism does not necessarily stop at the first zang-fu system acted upon. It may travel onward through the generating-and-overcoming network.

We still need restraint in interpretation. At present, we can say that “after a Fire-overcomes-Metal field configuration, Water- and Wood-related pathomechanism routes appeared more often.” We cannot yet translate this directly into the claim that “Lung Metal must first be damaged, then Kidney Water dries up, then Liver Wood loses nourishment.” The medical-case Path data have not yet fully distinguished whether each downstream station represents deficiency, excess, injury, or compensation.

Even so, this already changes how 4+3 should be read.

When we encounter `4+3` in the future, it is not enough to write “resonance grade seven.” We should at least ask:

**Which two elements are involved? Which is the primary cluster and which the secondary cluster? Are they in a generating or overcoming relation? Where does the pathomechanism finally stop?**

This resonates with the *Neijing*’s repeated discussions of “excess causing harm and regulation through restraint,” “mother–child transmission,” and “victory and restoration.” Yunqi is not five static boxes. It is closer to a pathomechanism network capable of transmission, redirection, and compensation.

Understood in this way, the Five Movements and Six Qi truly return from calendrical knowledge to clinical pattern differentiation.
