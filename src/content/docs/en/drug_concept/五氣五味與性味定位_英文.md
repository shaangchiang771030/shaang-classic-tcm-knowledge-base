The **five qi and five flavors** are the most familiar entry into learning materia medica, but we stress that although they are important, they cannot by themselves decode the entire field. The five qi are cold, cool, neutral, warm, and hot; the five flavors are sour, bitter, sweet, pungent, and salty. Together they provide the first layer of localization in a basic spacetime map of a medicine.

The five qi concern temperature and Yin-Yang cold/heat. The five flavors involve the Five Phases, entry into the zang, supplementation/drainage, and fixed verbs within temporal models. Pungent can disperse, sour can gather, sweet can relax, bitter can consolidate or dry, and salty can soften. If these are treated only as sensory tastes, their meaning as qi-transformation functions is lost.

Using *Shichangpu* and *Huanglian* as examples, pungency in *Shichangpu* supports reasoning toward dispersal, fragrance, and opening the orifices; bitter-cold *Huanglian* can be reasoned toward drying dampness, draining fire, and clearing heat. But nature and flavor still cannot explain every indication and must be combined with bodily form, clarity/turbidity, season, and rules from the *Inner Canon*.
