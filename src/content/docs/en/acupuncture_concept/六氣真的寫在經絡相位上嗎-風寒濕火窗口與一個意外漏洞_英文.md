If the twelve channels can really be placed in one common phase coordinate system, the next natural question is whether the qi-transformational characteristics called Wind, Cold, Dampness, Dryness, and Fire are also distributed non-randomly.

In other words, **are certain phase bands more likely to express a particular qi-transformational character?**

After re-zeroing all twelve primary channels at their Jing-Well points, the current data do indeed show several striking windows.

## Several recurring phase bands

Dividing 0°–360° into 30° intervals, some of the more concentrated bands across the twelve primary channels are:

- 90°–120°: Cold about 76%
- 180°–210°: Wind about 77%
- 270°–300°: Dampness about 91%
- 300°–330°: Wind about 85%
- 330°–360°: Fire about 64%

The current Six-Qi scores are therefore not uniformly scattered along the channels.

Dampness at 270°–300° and Wind at 300°–330° are especially concentrated across multiple channels.

This is why we no longer treat the Six Qi merely as labels attached to channel or disease names, but begin to consider whether they may be properties of **phase windows**.

The more important discovery comes next.

## Why can we see Wind, Cold, Dampness, and Fire, but almost no Dryness or Summerheat?

If these truly were the “Six Qi,” there should be six categories.

Yet the current primary Six-Qi distribution across the twelve channels is roughly:

- Wind: 98 points
- Fire: 89 points
- Dampness: 58 points
- Cold: 55 points
- Summerheat: 9 points
- Dryness: 0 points

So “Dryness” is not missing because a chart failed to draw it. In this version of the model, **no acupoint has Dryness as its first primary label**.

Summerheat is also rare.

This is a gap that cannot be hidden.

## The problem may lie in the classification, not the channels

Re-examination shows that the current Master Data uses:

> Wind, Cold, Dampness, Dryness, Fire, Summerheat

This is actually closer to the vocabulary of the **six excesses**.

But the strict Six Qi of the Five Movements and Six Qi should be:

- Jueyin Wind-Wood
- Shaoyin Sovereign Fire
- Taiyin Damp-Earth
- Shaoyang Ministerial Fire
- Yangming Dryness-Metal
- Taiyang Cold-Water

That means **two Fires**, not one Fire plus Summerheat.

So the real question may not be “why do the channels lack Summerheat?” but whether two similar yet distinct classical classification systems were mixed during data modeling.

Six columns in a table do not automatically mean that those columns are already the classical Six Qi.

## Where do the current Six-Qi scores come from?

They are not single labels copied directly from a classic.

The current Six-Qi vectors are model-derived quantities created in an earlier research round by combining multiple signals, including:

- phase bands;
- best harmonic *n*;
- geometric role;
- cues from characters in acupoint names;
- existing symptom / Six-Qi annotations.

The highest and second-highest scores are then recorded as primary and secondary Six-Qi labels.

This is appropriate for exploration, but it must not be mistaken for independent ground truth.

For example, points containing the character “wind” almost all include Wind among their labels. That is a beautiful internal consistency result, but acupoint-name roots themselves contributed to the Six-Qi score in the earlier model, so it cannot yet serve as a fully independent blind validation.

## The next elegant experiment

Rather than continue fine-tuning the Six-Qi score of each acupoint, the most valuable next step is a **name-blind test**:

First, completely remove the Six-Qi signal contributed by the acupoint name.

Second, recompute Six-Qi vectors using only phase, harmonic, geometric role, and independent symptom data.

Third, freeze the results and only then reveal the names.

If “Fengchi,” “Fengfu,” “Fengmen,” “Fengshi,” “Bingfeng,” and “Yifeng” still cluster toward Wind, the evidence becomes much stronger.

At the same time, the six labels should be re-decided: are we studying the six excesses, or the strict Six Qi of Jueyin, Shaoyin, Taiyin, Shaoyang, Yangming, and Taiyang?

The most important conclusion of this article is therefore not that “phase has proven the Six Qi,” but that two things are simultaneously true:

**Qi-transformational clustering does appear in phase bands; yet the current Six-Qi labeling system is not clean enough.**

A good model does not hide its gaps. It lets those gaps point toward a more precise next experiment.

The next article begins from the best blind-validation entry point: acupoint names. Why is Fengchi called “Wind Pool” rather than something else? Are apparently poetic names describing terrain, flow, qi transformation, or a compressed code for a control system?
