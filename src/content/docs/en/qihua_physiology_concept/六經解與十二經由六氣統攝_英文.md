The “twelve channels” and the “six channels” are not two competing classifications. They operate at different levels of resolution. The twelve channels describe concrete Hand/Foot and zang-fu pathways; the six-channel model combines two channels sharing the same qi into a higher-order qi-transformation unit. Hence the statement: “There are twelve channels; the Six Qi govern them; two channels share one qi.”

Lung and Spleen are both Taiyin; Heart and Kidney are Shaoyin; Liver and Pericardium are Jueyin; Gallbladder and Sanjiao are Shaoyang; Stomach and Large Intestine are Yangming; Bladder and Small Intestine are Taiyang. The twelve channels can therefore be treated as a **pathway layer**, and the six channels as a **shared functional qi-transformation layer**.

The Three Yin and Three Yang have long been difficult to understand because the *Inner Canon* uses them in several dimensions. The same terms can therefore carry different definitions in different temporal and spatial contexts.

As qi of Heaven, they describe properties of Heavenly Administration, Terrestrial Effect, and host/guest qi. When the energies of the Six Qi are understood as shaping human physiology—somewhat like different resonance modes within the cavity of a violin—the resulting high-level physiological organization is the six-channel framework on which Zhang Zhongjing based the first half of the *Shang Han Za Bing Lun*.

The six-channel system is broad because its conceptual basis can be traced to the *Suwen: Liu Jie Zang Xiang Lun*, which divides a full circular cycle into six phase intervals. In the resonance model explored here, the spacing between these modes is compared with musical interval structure, using a factor of roughly 2^(1/6) between adjacent steps (see the Bian Que Mirror Classic section).

The six channels can therefore be discussed in terms of Five-Phase properties, concrete branch pathways—the channels and collaterals—and functional resonance modes in Heaven, Earth, and the human body, expressed through Wind, Heat, Summerheat, Dampness, Dryness, and Cold. In the human body they also occupy distinct spatial regions. Chapters on the channels and skin regions describe surface territories, while other passages imply differences in depth.

Why define so many dimensions? The proposal here is that the natural system itself may require multiple coordinates. The ancients could compress it only until the model remained consistent with the phenomena they were trying to describe; beyond that point, further simplification would lose structure.

## Original Passage from *Exploring the Sources of Medicine*

“Heaven has Six Qi: Wind, Heat, Summerheat, Dampness, Dryness, and Cold. Earth has Five Phases: Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, and Water. … The six zang and six fu give rise to the twelve channels. Channel qi is rooted internally in the zang-fu and connects externally with the limbs and joints. … The Spleen and Lung channels are Taiyin; the Heart and Kidney channels are Shaoyin; the Liver and Pericardium channels are Jueyin; the Gallbladder and Sanjiao channels are Shaoyang; the Stomach and Large Intestine channels are Yangming; the Bladder and Small Intestine channels are Taiyang. There are twelve channels and the Six Qi govern them; two channels share one qi, therefore they are also called the six channels. Taiyang and Shaoyin are interior–exterior paired; Yangming and Taiyin are paired; Shaoyang and Jueyin are paired.”
