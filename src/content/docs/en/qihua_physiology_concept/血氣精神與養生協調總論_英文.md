Within this framework, health does not mean maximizing one function. It means maintaining coordination among Blood, Qi, Essence, Spirit, the channels, defensive qi, intention/will, and the balance of Cold and Warmth. When Blood is harmonious, the channels flow; when defensive qi is harmonious, the skin and flesh are nourished; when intention and will are harmonious, Spirit is focused and the *hun* and *po* do not disperse; when Cold and Warmth are harmonized, the Six Fu transform food and the channels remain open.

The center of “nourishing life” is therefore not one-way supplementation but maintaining **harmony, communication, appropriateness, and rhythm across several living subsystems**.

The traditional Daoist rules for preserving the four treasures—Spirit, Blood, Qi, and Essence—can be summarized as follows:

- **Nourish Spirit:** cultivate emptiness and stillness, hold to the One, practice “fasting of the mind” and sitting in forgetfulness. Spirit is the sovereign.
- **Nourish Qi:** concentrate qi and become supple; breathe deeply and evenly; store before expending. Qi and respiration act as a pivot.
- **Nourish Essence:** moderate sexual expenditure, store and seal, and avoid unnecessary leakage or depletion.
- **Nourish Blood:** Blood moves with Qi; support Spleen and Stomach so that Blood can be generated.

Looking back at modern life, constant internet and AI stimulation may overconsume attention and Spirit; rapid social tempo may disrupt the qi dynamic; excessive eating may impair rather than improve Blood movement. It is therefore reasonable, within this framework, to think about longevity partly in terms of how strongly a person’s daily schedule and temperament run against natural rhythms.

## Original Passage from *Exploring the Sources of Medicine*

“What Heaven gives us is virtue; what Earth carries for us is qi. When virtue flows and qi gathers, life is born. What arrives with life is called Essence; when two Essences contend and unite, this is called Spirit; that which follows Spirit in coming and going is called the *hun*; that which enters and exits alongside Essence is called the *po*. That which takes responsibility for things is called the Heart. Those who preserve life must follow the Four Seasons and adapt to Cold and Heat, harmonize joy and anger and settle their dwelling, regulate Yin and Yang and adjust firmness and softness. … Blood, Qi, Essence, and Spirit are what support life and circulate through one’s nature and destiny. The channels are what conduct Blood and Qi, manage Yin and Yang, moisten sinews and bones, and benefit the joints. Defensive qi warms the divided flesh, fills the skin, enriches the interstices, and governs opening and closing. Intention and will govern Spirit, gather the *hun* and *po*, adapt to Cold and Warmth, and harmonize joy and anger. Therefore when Blood is harmonious, the channels flow; when defensive qi is harmonious, the flesh separates freely and the skin is moist; when intention and will are harmonious, Spirit is concentrated and upright and the *hun* and *po* do not disperse; when Cold and Warmth are harmonious, the Six Fu transform grain, Wind impediment does not arise, the channels flow, and the limbs and joints are at ease.”
