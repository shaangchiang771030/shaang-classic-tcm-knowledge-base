Nutritive qi is described as a continuous circulatory system moving within the vessels. The ancients further linked it with breathing, pulse movement, total channel length, and day–night time. Nutritive qi begins at dawn in the Yin hour at the cun-kou of the Hand Taiyin, passes successively through the twelve channels, joins the Extraordinary Vessels, and then begins a new circuit. It completes fifty circuits in one day and night.

The important point is not the number “fifty” by itself. The model attempts to integrate respiratory rhythm, pulse events, pathway length, and whole-body circulation into a life-rhythm system that can be timed. On this model, one nutritive-qi circuit takes roughly 28.8 minutes. Its time scale therefore should not be confused with the popular twelve-double-hour/twelve-channel timetable of *ziwu liuzhu*. In the *Inner Canon*, different time scales can coexist in a fractal-like hierarchy without contradicting one another.

This mechanism is also important for the left/right cun–guan–chi pulse methods associated with the Bian Que tradition. Qi from all twelve channels can couple into the very short three-*cun* region of the radial pulse. Because fingertip discrimination is limited, later traditions simplified this into combinations of **upper/middle/lower × depth**, allowing the twelve channel signals to be separated only coarsely.

The pulse systems transmitted through the *Nan Jing* and later the *Mai Jing* are, in this sense, simplified versions. A real coupled physiological system would be denser. This helps explain why the *Mirror Classic of Bian Que* divides cun–guan–chi further into twenty-seven “observational positions” (*hou*): each of the twelve channels has three potential measurement positions across each of the three pulse regions. From the perspective of wave theory, that denser map may be closer to the complexity of the modeled system, although it is far less practical for ordinary fingertips.

## Original Passage from *Exploring the Sources of Medicine*

“When food and drink enter the Stomach, they transform and generate Qi and Blood. The swift and vigorous part of qi travels outside the vessels and is called defensive qi; the refined and concentrated part of Blood travels within the vessels and is called nutritive qi. Nutritive qi circulates fifty times around the body in one day and night. When a person exhales the pulse moves twice; when inhaling it moves twice; when the breath settles, five movements are counted, with the great breath added as an intercalation; with six movements, one breath of six movements is a normal pattern. One movement travels one cun; six movements travel six cun. Nutritive qi normally begins at dawn in the Yin hour at the cun-kou of Hand Taiyin, pours from Hand Taiyin into Hand Yangming, then Foot Yangming, Foot Taiyin, Hand Shaoyin, Hand Taiyang, Foot Taiyang, Foot Shaoyin, Hand Jueyin, Hand Shaoyang, Foot Shaoyang, and Foot Jueyin, ending in the two Qiao vessels, Du, and Ren; this completes one circuit. The twenty-eight vessels circulate again and again, Yin and Yang penetrating one another like a ring without end. When fifty circuits are complete, at the next dawn in the Yin hour it again meets at the cun-kou. This is the measure of nutritive qi.”
