At the end of the fifth series, “Tongtian” reconnects systems that previously seemed dispersed by listing a series of “seas”:

> Sanjiao is the Sea of Qi; the vessel pathways are the Sea of Blood; the Bao-network is the Sea of Essence; the Brain is the Sea of intention and will; bone is the Sea of Marrow; the channels and networks are the Sea of True Qi; the streams and valleys are the Sea of Fluids; and the joints are the Sea of Sinews.

This is not merely the familiar “Four Seas” or one fixed classification. At different levels, the text establishes **centers of convergence and exchange**.

## The core meaning of “sea” is not size, but convergence

A sea works as a model because many streams flow into it and new circulation can arise from it. Sanjiao gathers and distributes qi; vessel pathways carry blood; the Bao-network relates to essence; streams and valleys gather fluids; and the joints connect with the movement network of the sinews.

“Sea” therefore resembles a functional convergence layer more than a simple anatomical cavity.

## Chongmai, Yangming, Daimai, and Dumai are also connected

The text continues:

> Chongmai is the Sea of Channels and Vessels; it governs seepage and irrigation of the streams and valleys and joins Yangming at the ancestral sinews. Yangming is the Sea of the Five Zang and Six Fu and the chief of the Twelve Channels; it governs moistening of the ancestral sinews, all of which belong to Daimai and connect with Dumai.

The Chong, Ren, Du, Qiao, and stream-valley systems discussed in the first four series thus reappear within the whole-body network. This shows that the *Mirror Classic* does not write the extraordinary vessels, Twelve Channels, zang-fu, fluids, and blood as mutually unrelated chapters. It repeatedly establishes sites of intersection.

## Where do the waves of the Six Channels come from?

The passage ends:

> The surging, entangling, and repeated movement of the Six Channels all arises from the governing and responding of sea qi, with shifting of high and low, entry and exit, cold and heat.

“Surging,” “entangling,” “returning,” “high and low,” “entry and exit,” and “shifting” are all dynamic terms. Changes in the Six Channels are therefore not six independent lines rising and falling separately, but the channel-level expression of exchange among multiple “seas.”

This sentence also prepares the next series: if the body is such a complex exchange network, how can pulse diagnosis determine where a change is occurring?

The *Mirror Classic* does not answer by looking only at three points called Cun, Guan, and Chi. It establishes the **Twenty-Seven Hou**.
