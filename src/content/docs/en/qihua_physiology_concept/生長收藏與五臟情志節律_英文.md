Heaven has the Four Seasons and the Five Phases, by which it gives rise to birth, growth, transformation, gathering, and storage, and thereby to Cold, Summerheat, Dryness, Dampness, and Wind. Human beings have the Five Zang, which transform the five qi and give rise to joy, anger, sorrow, thought, and fear.

That is the surface-level meaning. The functions of birth, growth, transformation, gathering, and storage are driven, in this framework, by the properties of the Five Phases.

At a deeper level, the Five Phases and the emotions form an interface between what Daoist language calls *xing* and *ming*—one’s nature and one’s life endowment. External relationships and events stimulate human beings and generate emotional states.

The human “five emotions” are described here as joy, anger, grief, pensiveness, fear, and fright. When not excessive, they are part of what allows us to feel alive, but they also consume the energetic resources of the zang. Historical medical cases even describe patients whose emotional state shifted in such a way that recovery occurred without medication.

The *Mirror Classic of Bian Que*, a text lost for many centuries, pushes the combinatorial treatment of emotions especially far.

> In the transformations of the emotions: joy with anger transforms into *duan*; joy with grief into softness; joy with pensiveness into delight; joy with fear into restraint; joy with fright into rising. Anger with grief transforms into urgency; anger with pensiveness into agitation; anger with fear into violence; anger with fright into disorder. Grief with pensiveness transforms into sorrow; grief with fear into discouragement; grief with fright into dispersal. Pensiveness with fear transforms into resentment; pensiveness with fright into constraint; fear with fright transforms into dread.

This suggests that in real life, compound emotions are normal rather than exceptional. Regulating them may therefore also require composite methods—more like blending colors on a palette than applying a single one-dimensional correction.
