We regard Zhu Jinzhong as a physician who placed great importance on clinical review. His experience with medicines is not merely theoretical classification but a set of selection perspectives tested through long-term medical cases. Its value lies in bringing materia-medica theory down to each entry point of real clinical decision-making.

The core principle is that although the entry points of pattern differentiation appear different, they should still be placed within one spacetime model. Yin-Yang differentiation, Five-Phase differentiation, medicinal-nature differentiation, supplementation/drainage, five-flavor differentiation, six-channel differentiation, defensive-qi/nutritive-blood, Triple-Burner differentiation, symptom differentiation, and disease-specific differentiation are not mutually isolated schools. They are different clinical angles on the same human qi-transformation system.

We note that Zhu Jinzhong valued cases and that many of his viewpoints are followed by evidence accumulated through his long medical career. This is important for constructing a “Chinese-medicine brain”: abstract labels alone can spin in place, but putting diagnostic perspective, classical basis, and clinical validation in the same table makes the model more calibratable.
