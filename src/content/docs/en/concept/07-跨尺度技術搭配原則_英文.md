Once a single model—such as the Five Phases or the Three Yin and Three Yang—has been grasped, practical Chinese medicine depends on coordinating these models **across scales**.
The principle of cross-scale model integration means superimposing temporal scales (year, month, day, hour) and spatial scales (the macro environment of Heaven and Earth and the micro environment of the human zang-fu) in a matrix-like fashion and calculating their interactions. From this, the model seeks to derive predictive power relevant to human life, culture, agriculture, and even epidemiology.
The principle behind this concept is **the superposition and interference of multidimensional spatiotemporal energy fluctuations**.
Everything in the world simultaneously participates in cycles of many different scales. An animal, for example, exists within the day-night cycle (daily scale), the alternation of the four seasons (annual scale), and larger celestial cycles beyond the Earth. Only with a cross-scale matrix perspective can one attempt to capture the total energetic effect at a particular spatiotemporal node.
A friend with experience in a special-forces unit once described recruit training that required winter exposure, late-night training, and immersion in an icy pool. In this interpretation, the training stacked three factors regarded as injurious to the kidneys, on top of his individual constitution; two weeks later he was sent to the hospital emergency department.
