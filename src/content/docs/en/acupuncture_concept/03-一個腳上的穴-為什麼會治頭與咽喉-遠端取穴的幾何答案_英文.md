What most surprises modern readers about acupuncture is often not local point selection but **distal point selection**.

Why does a point on the foot appear in a passage on headache? Why should a point near the ankle relate to the throat, neck, or even chest and abdomen?

Saying “because they are on the same channel” is still not enough.

There are many points on one channel. Why this particular position?

That is the second-level question the phase model tries to answer.

## Jinggu for headache: not proximity, but repeated local phase

Jinggu BL64 lies distally on the Foot Taiyang Bladder channel.

In the *Jiayi Jing*, it can correspond to head symptoms such as headache. Anatomically, the lateral foot and the head are obviously far apart.

But in the best-harmonic model, Jinggu lies near a node of n10. Under the same n10 mode, several positions with similar local phase recur along the channel.

These same-phase positions form a “same-phase point family,” including Heyang, Weizhong, Yinmen, Danshu, Luoque, Tongtian, Chengguang, Wuchu, and Meichong at very different heights of the body.

When the textual indication domains of this family are united, they indeed include the **vertex, forehead, and brain**.

RULE_05, equal-distance phase equivalence, therefore gives an answer different from anatomical proximity:

> A distal point may relate to the head not because the two are physically close, but because they occupy similar local phases of the same spatial mode.

This is **phase proximity**, not centimeter proximity.

## Taixi for throat pain: crossing a lobe while retaining relative position

Now consider Taixi KI3.

Its *Jiayi Jing* indications include throat pain.

In the model, Taixi’s best geometric relation is not simple equal phase but RULE_04, **cross-node / cross-lobe symmetry**. In a corresponding wave lobe it finds geometric partners such as Zhaohai KI6, and the predictive domain generated by this cross-lobe relation contains the **throat, neck, and nape**.

Importantly, in this case RULE_05’s same-phase prediction does not fully recover the throat domain. The cross-lobe rule restores it.

So cross-lobe structure is not added merely to make the model more complicated. It handles a very specific phenomenon:

**The same function may recur not only within the same wave lobe. After crossing a node, the relative position in a corresponding lobe may still preserve a control relation.**

## Qiuxu for neck swelling: the same “clock angle” in different wave lobes

Qiuxu GB40 is another intuitive example.

Its indications include swelling and pain of the neck. When the model places Qiuxu into the local phase of n5, it finds phase-similar control positions such as Zhongdu, Daimai, Jianjing, and Wangu.

These points range from the lower limb through the trunk to the head and neck. Their anatomical positions differ greatly, but within a local wavelength they fall at similar “clock-face angles.”

The union of indication domains from this same-phase family includes the throat/neck region, recovering the neck-swelling passage for Qiuxu.

If one looks only at anatomical location, the relation appears scattered; in local phase, it compresses into one geometric family.

## Three more examples across distance

The same method also shows:

- **Quchi LI11 → pain of the shoulder and elbow**: cross-lobe symmetry restores an upper-limb control domain;
- **Chengjin BL56 → soreness and heaviness of the foot**: a cross-lobe relation includes the distal foot domain;
- **Fushe SP13 → hernia/masses**: the union of the equal-phase family includes the lower-abdomen/genital domain.

These examples do **not** mean that geometric partner points are necessarily interchangeable in treatment. The model says only:

**They share certain spatial control domains in the ancient indication data, and this sharing can be predicted from phase relationships.**

Whether they are therapeutically interchangeable, and which point is most effective to stimulate, are separate clinical questions.

## Distal may not be “distal” in phase space

We normally define near and far by centimeters on the body surface.

But if the channel system has some periodic spatial organization, another distance becomes important: **phase distance**.

In this coordinate system, foot and head can be anatomically very far apart but very close in a particular harmonic mode. Two adjacent points can likewise be nonequivalent in phase if they occupy different control roles.

This viewpoint does not require us to first believe in an invisible physical wave.

It offers a testable proposition:

> If distal point selection truly follows a geometric grammar, then given only acupoint phase and harmonic information—without knowing the point name or traditional explanation—we should recover its controlled spatial domain more often than chance.

The current retrospective data suggest that this direction deserves further testing.
