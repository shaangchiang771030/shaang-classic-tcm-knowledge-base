The Yin-Yang model not only governs temporal fluctuations horizontally; it also shapes spatial structure vertically. At the micro-spatial scale of the human body, the ancients established an orderly geometric method of division: the **human Yin-Yang spatial geometric symmetry model**. When the entire body is treated as a Yin-Yang system, its most important geometric boundary and point of symmetry is the **navel**, the Shenque acupoint.
The core first-principles idea is **topological partitioning with respect to a geometric center**. A tree offers an analogy: the depth of its roots is not necessarily less than the height of the tree above ground. A tree therefore also lives at the intersection of the qi of Heaven and Earth. In the human body, if the navel is taken as the horizontal axis of a geometric plane, a regular Yin-Yang spatial distribution appears: upper is Yang and lower is Yin; front (abdomen) is Yin and back is Yang; lateral is Yang and medial is Yin. Together these form a three-dimensional topological coordinate system.
What appears to us as the place where a tree meets the ground can, by analogy, be regarded as the tree's “navel.”
Within the classical framework, this geometric distribution allows the **principle of superposition** to be used in estimating the Yin-Yang attribute of any point on the body. For example, a point that is in the upper, posterior, and lateral region can be described as Yang within Yang within Yang.
