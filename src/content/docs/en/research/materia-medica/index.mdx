### From “What Does This Drug Treat?” Back to “Why Can This Drug Do It?”

The most common way to learn materia medica today is to attach a long list of functions to each drug: supplement qi, strengthen the Spleen, dry dampness, promote urination, stop sweating, calm the fetus, and so on. Such summaries are convenient, but they create a serious problem. When every herb has a dozen functions and every symptom can be treated by dozens of herbs, clinical selection ultimately falls back on memory, habit, or accumulated experience.

The Materia Medica domain tries to recover a deeper question: **why can a medicinal substance do what it does?** What relatively stable properties does the substance itself possess? And how do those properties generate different clinical effects when they enter different bodies, pathomechanisms, doses, and combinations?

The first stage therefore does not teach individual herbs. It explains why modern materia medica became so crowded. [The Herbal-Medicine Fishing Net and Returning to the Source](/en/drug_concept/01-本草漁網與正本清源/) describes the “fishing-net” effect created as historical materia-medica literature accumulated ever more indications. [Three-Dimensional Symbolic Convergence in Bencao Qiuzhen](/en/drug_concept/02-本草求真的三維符號化收斂/) begins searching for dimensions that can compress that material again. [The Rise of Zang-Fu Pattern Differentiation and the Retreat of the Three Yin and Three Yang](/en/drug_concept/03-臟腑辨證興起與三陰三陽退隱/) looks back at how the rise of zang-fu pattern differentiation partially obscured earlier qi-transformation and Three-Yin/Three-Yang contexts. [The Six Excesses, Phlegm, Accumulation, Qi and Blood, and the Priority of Root and Branch](/en/drug_concept/04-六淫痰積氣血與標本主次/) distinguishes causes, intermediate products, and branch manifestations among wind, cold, dampness, heat, phlegm, accumulation, qi, and blood. [Multi-Label Medicines and the Dilemma of Formula Selection](/en/drug_concept/05-多標籤藥物與選方困境/) carries the resulting multi-label problem into formula selection, while [The Shortcut and Limits of Symptom- and Disease-Specific Pattern Differentiation](/en/drug_concept/09-症狀專病辨證的捷徑與限制/) shows both the convenience and the limits of selecting “commonly used herbs” from a symptom or disease name alone.

Only after defining that problem do we enter the medicinal substance itself. [General Framework for Decoding the Shennong Bencao Jing](/en/drug_concept/12-神農本草經破解總綱/) proposes the general method: do not treat the *Shennong Bencao Jing* as hundreds of isolated efficacy cards; search instead for the stable dimensions the text uses to describe medicinal ontology. [The Image within Drug Names and Reasoning from Nomenclature](/en/drug_concept/13-藥名之象與命名推理/) examines whether names preserve clues about morphology, habitat, function, or qi transformation. [The Five Qi, Five Flavors, and Locating Medicinal Nature and Flavor](/en/drug_concept/14-五氣五味與性味定位/) returns to the familiar system of qi and flavor, while [Bodily Form and Treating Form through Form](/en/drug_concept/15-形體之體與以形治形/) brings root, stem, branch, bark, seed, fruit, and bodily form back into the model. [Ascent, Descent, Floating, Sinking, and the Four Seasons](/en/drug_concept/16-升降浮沉與春夏秋冬/) examines ascent, descent, floating, sinking, and seasonal direction. [Clarity, Turbidity, Density, and Speed of Onset](/en/drug_concept/17-清濁密度與起效速度/) considers clarity, turbidity, density, depth, texture, and speed of onset. [Harvest Season and the Endowment of Heaven and Earth](/en/drug_concept/18-採收時節與天地禀賦/) finally reminds us that when a substance is harvested and which part is used may themselves be part of its medicinal nature.

By this stage a different view of drugs should begin to form: **a drug is not a list of functions but a multidimensional vector.** Flavor, thermal nature, form, part used, direction, clarity/turbidity, season, and naming clues constitute a relatively stable ontology. “Treat cough,” “stop diarrhea,” or “remove vexation” are often downstream clinical results when that ontology interacts with a concrete pathomechanism.

How can we know these interpretations are more than elegant retrospective stories? The third stage therefore turns to causality and cross-validation. [Xu Lingtai's Method of Reading Causal Arrows](/en/drug_concept/19-徐靈胎因果箭頭解讀法/) trains the reader to reconstruct causal arrows in Xu Lingtai's explanations: because a substance has a certain property, it changes a particular pathological process, and a clinical effect follows. [An Inner Canon Symptom Rule Base and Explaining One Classic through Another](/en/drug_concept/21-內經病症規則庫與以經解經/) provides another classical evidence chain by connecting materia-medica descriptions with disease location, disease nature, and symptom rules in the *Inner Canon*. Only then does [AI-Assisted Extraction of Causal Reasoning from Ancient Texts](/en/drug_concept/06-ai輔助古籍因果提取法/) become useful: AI is not asked to invent drug properties, but to extract already-defined causal nodes from large bodies of classical text. [Validating a Classification Framework for the Principles of Materia Medica](/en/drug_concept/20-本草綱領的分類框架驗證/) asks whether the same dimensions reappear in another classificatory framework, and [Zhang Zhicong's Bencao Chongyuan and Extracting Innate Endowment](/en/drug_concept/22-張志聰本草崇原與禀賦提煉/) uses Zhang Zhicong's interpretation to test whether many surface-level functions can converge again onto fewer and more stable medicinal endowments.

Abstract methods must then survive pressure from real drugs. [Atractylodes: Supplementing Spleen-Earth and Layering Root and Branch](/en/drug_concept/23-白朮補益脾土與標本分層/) asks whether “supplementing the Spleen” is an intrinsic property or a downstream result. [Licorice: The Five Zang and Six Fu and Its Relatively Unbiased Nature](/en/drug_concept/24-甘草五臟六腑與不偏之性/) tests whether a drug with an apparently very broad range can still possess a coherent core. [Cinnamon Twig: Branch Morphology as a Channel for Communication of Qi Above and Below](/en/drug_concept/25-桂枝枝形通道與上下氣交通/) examines whether branch morphology and qi movement correspond. [Scutellaria: Yangming, Taiyin, and the Scope of Heat Clearing](/en/drug_concept/26-黃芩陽明太陰與清熱範圍/) asks what precise scope lies behind the overly broad label “clearing heat.” [Aconite and Asarum: Root-Branch Relations of Shaoyin and Taiyang](/en/drug_concept/27-附子細辛與少陰太陽標本/) studies one medicinal role across different disease positions and root-branch layers, while [Kudzu and Eucommia: Derivation through Morphology and Channels](/en/drug_concept/28-葛根杜仲與形態經脈推演/) reconnects morphology, channels, and concrete symptoms. These are not six unrelated drug stories; they are stress tests of the same ontology.

Once single-drug ontology becomes more reliable, [The Spacetime Distribution of the 360 Medicines of the Shennong Bencao Jing](/en/drug_concept/29-神農本草三百六十味的時空分佈/) can place the 360 medicines into a common spacetime distribution. They are no longer 360 disconnected points. We can ask which drugs cluster, which share surface indications but differ in underlying direction, which regions of the model are densely populated, and which remain sparse. A global distribution turns single-drug analysis into a system-level materia medica.

The genuinely clinical problem belongs near the end. [The Inner Canon Spacetime Model as the Master Coordinate System for Materia Medica](/en/drug_concept/07-內經時空模型作為藥物總座標/) places human pathomechanism and drug vectors into a shared coordinate system. [Zhu Jinzhong's Thirty-Three Clinical Perspectives on Drug Use](/en/drug_concept/08-朱進忠臨床用藥三十三視角/) reminds us that drug choice is influenced not only by disease name but by position, nature, time, constitution, accompanying patterns, and other conditions. [Dose-Based Pattern Differentiation and Dosage Design in Classical Formulas](/en/drug_concept/10-藥量辨證與經方劑量設計/) then makes dosage itself a control parameter: choosing the correct drug does not mean that the formula has already been determined. [Proportional Pattern Differentiation and Functional Layers within a Formula](/en/drug_concept/11-比例辨證與方中作用層位/) raises the problem from individual dosage to the relative weights and functional layers among drugs in a formula.

Finally, [Choosing among Physicians and the Criterion of Classical Self-Consistency](/en/drug_concept/30-醫家取舍與經典自洽判準/) asks what to do when physicians disagree. The answer cannot simply be to choose the master one prefers. Claims should be weighed against classical evidence, cross-text consistency, pathomechanistic coherence, and the ability to explain actual differences among formulas.

After all thirty articles, the reader should gradually leave behind the one-layer chain “symptom → find a drug” and acquire a fuller derivation:

**What endowment does the drug itself possess → which pathomechanistic nodes can that endowment act upon → which node is actually abnormal in this patient → why choose this drug among several candidates → why this dose → why this relative proportion with the other drugs?**

At that point materia medica stops being an enormous pharmacological memory task and becomes a discipline that can be reasoned through: from medicinal ontology to pathomechanism, from pathomechanism to prescription, and then from the prescription back to a test of whether our model of the drug was correct.
