The final question-and-answer discussion returns to a practical problem: when different physicians disagree about the same medicinal substance, how should one choose among them? The answer is **not** to follow whoever is more famous, but to examine whether the reasoning is concise, self-consistent, traceable to the classics, and clinically grounded.

Three criteria can be used. First, **can the essence be explained with a small amount of language?** If something that should take one or two sentences still remains unclear after ten, understanding may not be deep enough. Second, **is the work self-consistent from beginning to end?** If hundreds of pages frequently contradict one another, the system is insufficiently coherent. Third, **are the rules used for clinical derivation drawn from classics such as the *Inner Canon* and *Shanghan Zabing Lun*, rather than being invented at will?**

We also discuss statistics of the five flavors: in the *Shennong Bencao Jing*, medicines assigned two or more flavors are relatively rare. This may suggest that ancient drug selection valued relatively orthogonal coverage by each medicine. In formula composition, each drug ideally plays a distinct role like the X, Y, and Z axes, avoiding excessive overlap of functional ranges.
