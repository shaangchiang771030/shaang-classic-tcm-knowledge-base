The twelve channels are not twelve disconnected lines. They form a continuous route in which one segment joins the next. Nutritive qi follows this sequence, then connects with vessels such as the Du and Ren, producing a closed circulation in which “Yin and Yang penetrate one another, like a ring without end.” The key is not memorizing twelve channel names but understanding that their intersections turn local pathways into one circulatory transport network. The sequence corresponds to the traditional twelve double-hours and twelve channels of the *ziwu liuzhu* system and need not be repeated here.

This order of circulation also matches the three paired axes of root–manifestation–central qi discussed earlier—Water/Fire, Dryness/Dampness, and Wind/Ministerial Fire. Within this research interpretation, that correspondence again suggests that the channel network may preserve structural traces of the Six-Qi organization.

Careful readers will notice that the classics preserve two different values for the total length of the channels. The *Ling Shu* version is shorter and does not include the Extraordinary Vessels, totaling roughly sixteen *zhang* and two *chi*. The *Mirror Classic of Bian Que* gives a total of twenty-two *zhang*, three *chi*, and six *cun*.

The two versions are also associated with different total numbers of breaths over one day and night. The former gives 13,500 breaths, while the *Mirror Classic* gives 18,780. We can only say that the parameters in the *Ling Shu* are closer to a slower respiratory model, whereas the *Mirror Classic* resembles a more ordinary population-level respiratory rate.

Why do the classics care about total length and the phrase “like a ring without end”? Nutritive qi is described as moving relatively slowly compared with modern blood flow. From a physics analogy, one might compare the relevant propagation to phase-like movement rather than bulk fluid transport. Nutritive qi represents the circulation of the “human qi” and is also related to rules such as prohibited needling. Because the pathway forms a closed loop, a traveling-wave analogy allows continued circulation with relatively little loss.

Different channels, however, are described as having different natural ratios of Qi and Blood. Within the resonance hypothesis explored in this project, different characteristic modes would create impedance-matching problems between channels, allowing standing-wave patterns to arise and different acupoints to take on different functional properties.

The duality between traveling and standing patterns is described in the *Mirror Classic of Bian Que* with the terms “flow” and “pouring/registration” (*liu zhu*), rather than being an invention of later interpretation:

> “Flow is to irrigate and moisten; pouring is to circle and return.”

## Original Passage from *Exploring the Sources of Medicine*

“From the Yin hour the Lung begins, and by the Chou hour the Liver is reached. The Hand Taiyin Lung vessel begins in the Middle Burner … and meets the Hand Yangming Large Intestine. The Hand Yangming Large-Intestine vessel … meets the Foot Yangming Stomach vessel. The Foot Yangming Stomach vessel … meets the Foot Taiyin Spleen vessel. The Foot Taiyin Spleen vessel … meets the Hand Shaoyin Heart vessel. The Hand Shaoyin Heart vessel … meets the Hand Taiyang Small-Intestine vessel. The Hand Taiyang Small-Intestine vessel … meets the Foot Taiyang Bladder vessel. The Foot Taiyang Bladder vessel … meets the Foot Shaoyin Kidney vessel. The Foot Shaoyin Kidney vessel … meets the Hand Jueyin Pericardium. The Hand Jueyin Pericardial vessel … meets the Hand Shaoyang Sanjiao. The Hand Shaoyang Sanjiao vessel … meets the Foot Shaoyang Gallbladder vessel. The Foot Shaoyang Gallbladder vessel … meets the Foot Jueyin Liver vessel. The Foot Jueyin Liver vessel … meets the Hand Taiyin Lung vessel.”

“The movement of nutritive qi normally begins at dawn in the Yin hour, starting from the cun-kou of the Hand Taiyin. From Hand Taiyin it pours into Hand Yangming, then Foot Yangming, Foot Taiyin, Hand Shaoyin, Hand Taiyang, Foot Taiyang, Foot Shaoyin, Hand Jueyin, Hand Shaoyang, Foot Shaoyang, and Foot Jueyin, ending in the two Qiao vessels, Du, and Ren; this is one circuit. The twenty-eight vessels circulate again and again, Yin and Yang penetrating one another like a ring without end.”
