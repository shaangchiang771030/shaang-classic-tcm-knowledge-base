Root, manifestation, and central qi form the key interface by which the preceding twelve-channel, six-channel, and Six-Qi models enter clinical pathology. Wind, Cold, Summerheat, Dampness, Dryness, and Fire are the **root**; the Three Yin and Three Yang are the **manifestation**; and the qi of the paired interior–exterior channel forms the **central qi**.

The six channels do not all become diseased according to the same rule. Shaoyang and Taiyin mainly follow the root; Shaoyin and Taiyang may follow either root or manifestation; Yangming and Jueyin may follow central qi. Thus the same six-channel category can produce very different patterns of Cold, Heat, Dryness, Dampness, Wind, or Fire depending on root, manifestation, central qi, and the direction of transformation.

When this structure is connected with the six-channel transmission sequence described in the *Treatise on Heat*, it creates a complete external-pathogen pathomechanistic chain:

**Six Qi → six channels → root / manifestation / central qi → transformation → channel location → symptoms and transmission.**

The root–manifestation–central-qi model comes especially from the *Suwen: Liu Wei Zhi Da Lun* and *Zhi Zhen Yao Da Lun*. From the same Heaven–Human perspective, it has direct diagnostic use.

A. The two qi linked through an interior–exterior pair behave somewhat like a seesaw: when one side becomes stronger, the other becomes relatively weaker. The ancients therefore used images such as “the six channels surging like waves” to describe channel qi rising and falling. The *Ling Shu: Zhong Shi* pulse method comparing the Renying and Cun-kou, with one-, two-, and three-fold exuberance, is one example of measuring paired exterior/interior qi.

B. Root–manifestation–central qi adds constraints to diagnosis. More observations can then be explained by a smaller number of pathomechanistic hypotheses.

C. It also makes otherwise puzzling textual structures more reasonable—for example, why a Taiyang chapter may contain a Shaoyin formula, or a Shaoyin chapter a Taiyang formula.

## Original Passage from *Exploring the Sources of Medicine*

“If the manifestation, root, and central qi of the six channels are not understood, one cannot read the *Shang Han* texts; therefore they are explained here in detail for study. … Above Shaoyang, Fire qi governs, and Jueyin is seen within it … above Yangming, Dryness qi governs, and Taiyin is seen within it … above Taiyang, Cold qi governs, and Shaoyin is seen within it … above Jueyin, Wind qi governs, and Shaoyang is seen within it … above Shaoyin, Heat qi governs, and Taiyang is seen within it … above Taiyin, Dampness qi governs, and Yangming is seen within it.”

“Shaoyang and Taiyin follow the root; Shaoyin and Taiyang follow root and manifestation; Yangming and Jueyin do not follow root or manifestation but follow central qi. … Shaoyin and Taiyang follow both root and manifestation because Shaoyin has Heat as root but Yin as manifestation, while Taiyang has Cold as root but Yang as manifestation. Their root and manifestation are different qi, so they may follow one or the other and treatment must examine this carefully. … Yangming and Jueyin, by contrast, follow central qi: the central qi of Yangming is Taiyin Dampness-Earth, so Dryness may transform by following Dampness; the central qi of Jueyin is Shaoyang Ministerial Fire, so Wood may transform by following Fire.”

“The *Inner Canon* states: on the first day of Cold Damage, Taiyang receives it; on the second, Yangming; on the third, Shaoyang; on the fourth, Taiyin; on the fifth, Shaoyin; on the sixth, Jueyin. This is where the theory of the six channels begins. … Its usual method of transmission is from exterior to interior and from shallow to deep, with an ordinary sequence. … Yet this is only the normal order and must not be applied rigidly. … People differ in the form and qi they have received, and pathogens likewise differ in the routes through which they transmit; one cannot bind every case to a single normal example.”
