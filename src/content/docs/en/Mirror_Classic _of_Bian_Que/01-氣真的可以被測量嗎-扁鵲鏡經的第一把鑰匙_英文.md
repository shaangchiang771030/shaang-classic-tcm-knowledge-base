The first thing to grasp in the *Mirror Classic of Bian Que* is not the Twelve Lü, nor the unfamiliar sequence “An, Fu, Gong, Ge, Zhi, Ling, Jie, Bian, Sui, He.” The real starting point is more fundamental: **can invisible “qi” actually be observed?**

The text does not begin by giving qi an abstract definition. Instead, it links a series of phenomena that can be perceived by the human senses:

> A person’s tone is the lü of the movement of breath; a person’s breath governs the movement of the pulse; a person’s pulse is the manifestation of the movement of qi; a person’s qi makes clear the movement of voice; a person’s voice is the capacity of the movement of lü; and a person’s lü is the mirror of tone and voice.

This passage already states roughly half of the book’s method.

Qi itself cannot be seen directly, but once qi moves within the body, it leaves “traces.” Breathing can be faster or slower, the pulse rises and falls, the voice has higher and lower qualities, and the channels have flow and infusion. These phenomena are not isolated. The text’s method is to use a layer that is easier to observe to infer another layer.

## The “mirror” is more than a metaphor

The “mirror” in the title can first be understood as an **indirect observer**.

We cannot see wind itself, yet we know it is blowing from the motion of leaves. We cannot see electric current directly, yet we can know its changes from a meter reading. Similarly, the *Mirror Classic* holds that although qi cannot be directly seen, it leaves comparable “manifestations” in breathing, pulse movement, voice, and bodily locations.

Thus the text also says:

> The pulse is the repository of blood and the path of qi. The transport of the pulse is breathing. The qi of the pulse is carried by blood. The path of the pulse is the movement of qi.

At least four layers are established here: **the movement of qi, the carrying function of blood, the trace formed as pulse, and the rhythm of breathing**. They are not the same thing, yet they affect one another within a single dynamic system.

## What most distinguishes the *Mirror Classic* from generic “image-and-number” thinking

If one only says that “Heaven and humanity correspond” or that “qi travels through the whole body,” the discussion can remain conceptual. What is distinctive in the *Mirror Classic* is that it immediately asks a further question: what can be measured?

The text states:

> What can be measured are the divisions of bone measure and the positions of the skin regions.

It then links the cycle of exhalation and inhalation, the number of pulse movements, the distance traveled by qi, the Twelve Lü, Hou, and She layer by layer. In other words, it is not satisfied with the idea that “qi exists”; it attempts to establish **scales by which normality and deviation can be compared**.

This is also the most important way to read the book: do not rush to translate every ancient term into a modern physiological term. First ask what measurement role it plays within the system.

## Once this is understood, the later terminology has a place

The rest of the book can be read as a progressive construction of measuring tools:

- **breath (xi)** provides a unit of time;
- **pulse movements** provide events within a cycle;
- **bone measure** provides an individualized length scale;
- **tone (yin)** describes the state of qi;
- **lü** describes the regulated measure of qi movement;
- **Shou** establishes the starting point of an interpretation;
- **She** locates where qi interaction resides;
- **Hou** provides observation windows;
- **Qiheng and Kuidu** only then judge normality and abnormality.

So the first key to the *Mirror Classic of Bian Que* is not memorizing terminology. It is accepting its basic method: **invisible qi is inferred from manifestations that can be seen, heard, palpated, and measured.**
