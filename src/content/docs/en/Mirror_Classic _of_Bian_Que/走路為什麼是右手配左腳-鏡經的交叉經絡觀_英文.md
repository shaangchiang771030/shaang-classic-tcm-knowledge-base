The beginning of the “Eight She” is unexpected.

It does not first list channels or acupoints. It begins with walking:

> When a person walks, the right hand advances in correspondence with the left foot, and the left hand with the right foot. Hands and feet alternately move forward; all this results from the comparative relations of channel qi. To compare means that two become one in harmony and govern and respond to one another.

Anyone can observe the phenomenon: during walking, upper and lower limbs commonly advance in contralateral coordination.

## Why begin with walking?

Because the central subject of the Eight She is not a single channel but **how different channels coordinate**.

Walking gives the reader an immediate demonstration that the right half of the body does not cooperate only with the right half, nor is the left half independent. There is cross-coordination among upper/lower and left/right.

The text uses the concepts of comparison and mutual governing-response to describe this relation.

## “Comparison” is more than simple similarity

The phrase is followed by “becoming one in harmony and governing and responding to one another.” It therefore describes functional coordination between locations.

This prepares the many pairings that appear later in the Eight-She table:

- right Hand Shaoyin concurrently with left Foot Taiyin;
- right Hand Yangming harmonizing with right Foot Shaoyang;
- left Hand Jueyin in parallel with left Foot Shaoyang;
- right Hand Jueyin overcoming left Foot Taiyin;

and so on.

These hand-foot channels are not randomly combined. The pairings presuppose that different positions can govern and respond to one another.

## The Six Channels form a network, not six parallel lines

The third series already presented Taiyin-Taiyang as warp and weft, Shaoyin-Shaoyang as pivot and sustaining, and Jueyin-Yangming as the four dimensions. In the Eight She, this abstract geometry becomes concrete as cross-pairings among left and right hands and feet.

The crucial question is therefore: **what relation forms when qi meets between two junctions?**

This also explains why the text uses relational terms such as Zhuan, Sheng, Jian, Bing, and He rather than simply declaring one channel deficient and another excessive.

## From contralateral walking to whole-body qi interaction

Walking is only the demonstration. The text immediately expands to the Twelve Channels and the 365 joints, saying that left-right qi and upper-lower positions can form She at the great meetings of Ying-Wei streams and valleys wherever qi interacts.

Many locations in the body can therefore become interaction nodes.

The “Eight She” are not eight fixed points. They are a model for how qi-transformational junctions combine, shift, and govern-response to one another.
