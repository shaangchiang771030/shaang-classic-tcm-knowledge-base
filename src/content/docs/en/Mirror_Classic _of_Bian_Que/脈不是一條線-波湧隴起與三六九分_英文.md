When the *Mirror Classic of Bian Que* discusses pulse, it does more than count how many beats occur per minute. “Qiheng” begins to describe the **geometry of pulse movement** directly.

The text says:

> The pulses of Taiyin and Taiyang surge nine fen and rise like a ridge; the pulses of Yangming and Jueyin surge three fen and rise like a ridge; the pulses of Shaoyin and Shaoyang surge six fen and rise like a ridge.

It then defines the terms explicitly:

> Surging is the high and low of oscillation. Ridge-rising is the magnitude of oscillation. The small is sharp and short; the large is broad and long.

## High-low and magnitude are two different dimensions

“Surging” describes high-low; “ridge-rising” describes magnitude. When breadth, sharpness, length, and shortness are added, pulse morphology becomes a multidimensional signal with at least amplitude, width, and shape.

This differs from merely labeling a pulse floating, deep, slow, or rapid. The text is closer to recording how a wave occupies the spatial region of the Qi Mouth.

## Three, six, and nine fen form Six-Channel baseline amplitudes

The text places Taiyin-Taiyang at nine fen, Shaoyin-Shaoyang at six, and Yangming-Jueyin at three.

These three scales recur later in the final chapters on individual types, so they are not incidental numbers.

The most defensible reading is to treat them as the *Mirror Classic*’s own **baseline divisions of oscillation**. Whether they can map directly onto millimeters of displacement, pulse pressure, or any other modern quantity requires separate experimental work.

## Pathology also depends on anterior-posterior shape

The text gives examples such as “larger behind and sharper in front” and “larger in front and sharper behind,” and even uses whether the pulse extends one, two, or three fen beyond a particular Hou to differentiate states.

Disease is therefore not judged only from “large or small.” One must see how the waveform extends anteriorly and posteriorly across the Qi Mouth.

## Pulse diagnosis in the Mirror Classic resembles morphological analysis

Putting the preceding dimensions together, one pulse examination may include at least:

**when movement begins, at which Hou it rises, how many fen it oscillates, whether the waveform is broad or sharp, whether it moves forward or backward, and in which respiratory phase it appears.**
