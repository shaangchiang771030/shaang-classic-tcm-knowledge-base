### Before Discussing Disease, Rebuild a Body That Rises, Descends, Enters, Exits, and Changes with Time

Modern readers often enter Chinese medicine through disease: Liver fire, Spleen deficiency, Kidney yang deficiency, Lung qi deficiency, or Six-Channel pattern differentiation. Classical medicine, however, poses a more basic question first: **how does a normal human being operate before disease appears?**

The Qi-Transformation Physiology domain attempts to reconstruct that layer. It does not merely reintroduce the five zang, six fu, and twelve channels as anatomical labels. It asks the reader to see the classical body as a dynamic system in which ascent and descent, entry and exit, separation of clear and turbid, communication of water and fire, generation-growth-gathering-storage, and exchange between interior and exterior are continuously occurring. The most coherent reading order therefore begins from the simplest principles of qi transformation and gradually “builds” a human body from them.

[Understanding Principles as a Physician: General Framework of Heaven-Human Qi Transformation](/en/qihua_physiology_concept/01-醫者明理與天人氣化總綱/) first explains why medicine must understand seasonal timing, qi transformation, deficiency and excess, and normal versus counterflow. [One Qi, Yin-Yang, Clear and Turbid, and the Pivot of Central Qi](/en/qihua_physiology_concept/02-一氣陰陽清濁與中氣樞軸/) then establishes the first working model: one qi differentiates into yin and yang, clear and turbid, while Central Qi functions as the pivot that prevents ascent and descent from separating into disconnected processes. [Four Images, Five Phases, and the Rhythm of Spring, Summer, Autumn, and Winter](/en/qihua_physiology_concept/03-四象五行與春夏秋冬節律/) expands yin-yang into the annual rhythm of generation, growth, gathering, and storage; [Spleen-Stomach Central Earth and the Generation of the Five Phases in the Body](/en/qihua_physiology_concept/04-脾胃中土與人體五行生成/) places that structure into the body and asks why Spleen-Stomach Earth may function as a pivot rather than merely one of five equivalent boxes. [Diagrammatic Exploration of Origins: A General Map of Heaven-Earth-Human Qi Transformation](/en/qihua_physiology_concept/05-探源圖解與天地人氣化總圖/) recombines those pieces into an overall diagram. [Signs of Water and Fire and the Pathways of Up, Down, Left, and Right](/en/qihua_physiology_concept/07-水火徵兆與上下左右道路/) then connects water-fire, up-down, and left-right dynamics to observable signs, while [Generation, Growth, Gathering, Storage, and the Emotional Rhythms of the Five Zang](/en/qihua_physiology_concept/08-生長收藏與五臟情志節律/) projects seasonal rhythm into zang physiology and emotion.

Because the human body is not closed, it always exists within a changing external temporal environment. [Temporal Governance of the Six Qi and Annual Climatic Segmentation](/en/qihua_physiology_concept/09-六氣主時與年度氣候分段/) first establishes the relatively stable sixfold rhythm of a year. [Sitian, Zaiquan, and the Climate of the Upper and Lower Halves of the Year](/en/qihua_physiology_concept/10-司天在泉與上下半年氣候/) explains Sitian and Zaiquan as climatic backgrounds of the upper and lower halves of the year. Only then does [Tianfu, Suihui, Taiyi Tianfu, and the Superposition of Movement and Qi](/en/qihua_physiology_concept/11-天符歲會太乙天符與運氣疊加/) examine what happens when annual movement, year branch, Sitian, and related conditions overlap and strengthen one another. The sequence matters: establish the baseline first, then study superposition. Without that order, the names of Five Movements and Six Qi remain little more than a table to memorize.

After the Heaven-Earth framework is established, [The Beginning of Human Life and the Formation of Ancestral Qi](/en/qihua_physiology_concept/12-人生之始與祖氣成形/) turns to the formation of an individual life. Concepts such as the joining of the two essences, undifferentiated ancestral qi, the five spirits, five qi, five zang, and five sensory structures are treated as stages in the differentiation of a human being who has both form and spirit, both inherited and acquired conditions.

[General Theory of the Functional Network of the Five Zang and Six Fu](/en/qihua_physiology_concept/13-五臟六腑功能網路總論/) then reminds us that zang and fu form a functional network rather than twelve isolated organs. Instead of memorizing them in a fixed list, the next articles follow a circulation of qi movement. [Spleen and Stomach: Central Transportation, Transformation, and Distribution from the Great Granary](/en/qihua_physiology_concept/16-脾胃中央運化與太倉輸布/) establishes the central pivot of Spleen and Stomach; [Liver and Gallbladder Wind-Wood: Planning, Upright Judgment, and Decision](/en/qihua_physiology_concept/15-肝膽風木謀慮與中正決斷/) examines Liver-Gallbladder Wind-Wood and the leftward/upward movement of planning and orderly expansion; [Heart and Small Intestine: Sovereign Fire, Spirit, Transformation of Matter, and Blood Generation](/en/qihua_physiology_concept/14-心與小腸君火神明與化物生血/) follows transformation toward Heart and Small-Intestine Sovereign Fire, spirit, and blood; [Lung and Large Intestine: Regulation, Diffusion-Descent, and Conduction](/en/qihua_physiology_concept/17-肺大腸制節宣降與傳導/) turns toward Lung-Large-Intestine regulation, diffusion, descent, and conduction; [Kidney and Bladder: Water Storage, Fluids, and the Entry-Exit of Qi Transformation](/en/qihua_physiology_concept/18-腎膀胱水藏津液與氣化出入/) returns to Kidney-Bladder storage, fluids, and lower entry-exit of qi transformation. [Pericardium and Sanjiao: Ministerial Fire, the Envoy, and the Waterway Membrane Network](/en/qihua_physiology_concept/19-心包三焦相火臣使與水道膜網/) handles Pericardium and Sanjiao as a whole-body distribution and waterway network that cannot be reduced to one organ, while [Zang-Fu Location, Form, and Internal-External Networking](/en/qihua_physiology_concept/20-臟腑部位形象與內外連網/) finally reconnects functional qi transformation to bodily form and location so that the model does not drift into pure abstraction.

Once zang-fu relations exist, we need to know how they are connected. [The Twelve Channels Intersecting in an Endless Ring](/en/qihua_physiology_concept/21-十二經相交與如環無端/) establishes the endless-ring network of the Twelve Channels. [Chong, Ren, Du, and Dai: The Meeting of Earlier and Later Heaven](/en/qihua_physiology_concept/22-衝任督帶與先後天交會/) adds the extraordinary-vessel architecture of Chong, Ren, Du, and Dai. Only at that point can [Explaining the Six Channels: Twelve Channels Governed by the Six Qi](/en/qihua_physiology_concept/23-六經解與十二經由六氣統攝/) explain that the Six Channels are not six of the Twelve Channels, but a higher-level mode of organizing them through Six-Qi dynamics. [Opening, Closing, and Pivoting of the Six Channels: A Key to Pattern Examination](/en/qihua_physiology_concept/29-六經開闔樞與審證大關鍵/) then develops opening, closing, and pivoting as movement roles of the Three Yin and Three Yang, and [Explaining the Six Qi: Temporal Governance, Transformation, and Rise and Decline of Channel Qi](/en/qihua_physiology_concept/24-六氣解與主令化氣盛衰/) connects the specific qualities and temporal governance of the Six Qi to this dynamic system.

Qi transformation also unfolds across multiple time scales. [Nutritive Qi Circulation: Fifty Circuits Through the Vessels Day and Night](/en/qihua_physiology_concept/25-營氣運行與脈中日夜五十周/) studies the fifty day-night circulations of nutritive qi within the vessels; [Defensive Qi Entry and Exit: Wake-Sleep and Protective Rhythms](/en/qihua_physiology_concept/26-衛氣出入與寤寐防衛節律/) shows how defensive qi, wakefulness, sleep, surface defense, and interior-exterior movement interlock. [Tiangui in Women and Men and the Long Cycle of Life](/en/qihua_physiology_concept/27-男女天癸與生命長周期/) expands the scale from a day to the human life course through Kidney qi, Tiangui, Chong-Ren, bones, sinews, and reproduction. [General Theory of Blood, Qi, Essence, Spirit, and Coordinated Health Preservation](/en/qihua_physiology_concept/28-血氣精神與養生協調總論/) then recombines blood, qi, essence, spirit, channels, defensive qi, and intention into a coordinated homeostatic system.

Precisely because so much effort has been spent constructing a model of the normal person, [Yin-Yang Transformation and Treating Disease by Seeking the Root](/en/qihua_physiology_concept/06-陰陽變化與治病求本/) is best read near the end rather than at the beginning. “Treat disease by seeking the root” can now mean more than “cold is treated with heat, heat with cold.” It becomes a layered question: Is ascent-descent disturbed? Has Central Qi lost its pivot? Are water and fire failing to communicate? Has a zang-fu network lost balance? Are Six-Channel opening, closing, and pivoting abnormal? Or is the external Six-Qi environment mismatched with the person's baseline? [Root, Manifestation, and Central Qi: Integrating Externally Contracted Pathomechanisms](/en/qihua_physiology_concept/30-本標中氣與外感病機整合/) then pushes that normal physiology into pathology by integrating root, manifestation, central qi, Six Channels, hidden qi, summerheat-dampness, and externally contracted disease.

After all thirty articles, the goal is not simply to memorize Five-Phase correspondences of the zang. The reader should be able to **run a classical physiological model mentally**: how qi rises and descends, how clear and turbid separate, how the center acts as pivot, how the zang-fu form a circulation, how the channels connect that circulation, how the Six Channels organize it at a higher level, how nutritive and defensive qi change across day and night, and how the organism is embedded in seasons and the life cycle.

Only then does pattern differentiation become more than attaching labels to symptoms. The central question becomes: **at what point did this person's entire order of qi transformation begin to deviate from normal ascent, descent, entry, and exit?**
