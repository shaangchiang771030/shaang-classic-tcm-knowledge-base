In the *Mirror Classic of Bian Que*, “Hou” and “She” are often written together as “Hou-She,” making it easy to assume they are near-synonyms.

At the end of the “Twenty-Seven Hou,” however, the text explicitly distinguishes them:

> She is the position reached by the meeting of Nourishing and Defensive qi; Hou is where pulse, breath, tone, and lü enter and leave.

This sentence is crucial.

## She: where qi interaction takes place

The earlier Eight-She discussion already defined She as the place of qi-transformational joint meeting. It asks where different channels, different Nine-Qi states, and true qi intersect, and how relations such as Zhuan, Sheng, Jian, Bing, and He are formed.

She therefore primarily describes **the position of qi interaction**.

## Hou: where we can observe it

Hou, by contrast, is called an “instrument for measuring pulse and breath” and is arranged across twenty-seven observational indices within Cun, Guan, and Chi.

Hou therefore primarily describes **the observation window**.

One qi-interaction event may leave signals at different Hou positions; the same Hou may reveal changes associated with different She as qi moves. The two are therefore not in a simple one-to-one relation.

## What does “one She, three Hou; one Hou, eight She” reveal?

The text says:

> One She has three Hou; one Hou has eight She, according to the high-low and entry-exit of tone and breath.

The most important point is not the number itself, but that the source explicitly acknowledges a **many-to-many mapping** between Hou and She.

This helps explain why directly assigning one pulse position to one zang-fu loses much of the information in the *Mirror Classic*. Interpretation requires knowing both where qi interaction is occurring as She and from which Hou it is currently being observed.

## This distinction turns diagnosis into an inverse problem

Clinically, what is palpated is pulse movement at the Hou position. What one actually wants to know is the deeper qi-interaction position and channel relationship.

The *Mirror Classic*’s workflow can therefore be summarized as:

**observe Hou → obtain tone-lü and oscillation → infer the She being reached → determine the qi-transformational relation.**

This is why the later methods of Qiheng and Kuidu must be built on Hou and She.
