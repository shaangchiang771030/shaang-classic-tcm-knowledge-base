The **annual Five-Phase generating-cycle model** divides the flow of one year linearly into five equal temporal phases in the sequence Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, and Water.
A 360-day year is divided evenly into five periods of 72 days each. The ancients condensed the functional characteristics of these phases into five verbs: **generate, grow, transform, gather, store**. Whenever the *Huangdi Neijing* lists the five seasons as spring, summer, long summer, autumn, and winter—with long summer explicitly included—we can identify the passage as using an annual Five-Phase model rather than a Four Images model.

The article interprets “generation, growth, transformation, gathering, and storage” through modern biological phenomena, applying the sequence to animals, plants, and fish. Summer belongs to Fire (growth), when animals are described as showing their most intense territorial and social behavior and therefore greater likelihood of conflict. **Transformation** is the critical conversion switch: it changes dynamic, easily dispersed energy such as blood glucose or nutrients into more stable structural energy such as starch, fat, sperm, and ova. Autumn belongs to Metal (gathering), when plants and animals begin storing energy and enter a key period of weight gain. Winter belongs to Water (storage), most vividly represented by animal hibernation.
For health preservation after middle age, the article suggests creating a “winter” within each day—allowing oneself to cool and settle—in order to obtain higher-quality sleep. This is also presented as an example of cross-scale application.
