### From Measurement and Encoding to Individualized Diagnosis: Rethinking Classical Qi

The first difficulty in studying the *Mirror Classic of Bian Que* is the density of unfamiliar terms: tones, lü pitch-pipes, hou observation positions, she stations, shou starting states, the Nine Qi, sixty shou, seventy-two she, twenty-seven hou, and many others. If these are memorized one by one, the text quickly turns into an enormous and obscure vocabulary. From another angle, however, these terms are not isolated. They repeatedly answer one coherent question: **how did the ancients turn invisible processes of qi transformation into something that could be observed, located, compared, and inferred?**

The book should therefore not be entered through disease names, nor by asking what disease one “tone” represents. The first task is measurement. [Can Qi Really Be Measured? The First Key to the Mirror Classic of Bian Que](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/01-氣真的可以被測量嗎-扁鵲鏡經的第一把鑰匙/) raises the most basic question of the whole project. [Between One Exhalation and One Inhalation: What Is One Baseline Breath?](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/02-一呼一吸之間-什麼叫平人一息/) establishes the body's own temporal baseline, and [Five Pulse Movements in One Breath, Qi Traveling Six Cun: Turning Qi Transformation into Numbers](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/03-一息脈五動氣行六寸-古人怎樣把氣化變成數字/) places breathing, pulse movement, and the distance traveled by qi into a common proportion. [A Cun Is Not a Centimeter: Bone Measure as the Body’s Own Ruler](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/04-寸不是公分-骨度如何成為人體自己的尺/) then reminds us that spatial measurement is not a fixed modern centimeter but a body-relative scale. From there, [From Six Cun to Twenty-Two Zhang, Three Chi, Six Cun: How Qi Completes One Circuit](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/05-從六寸到二十二丈三尺六寸-氣如何走完人體一周/) extends one local breath to a whole-body circulation, while [Why Must the Body Keep Time with the Day? Twelve Double-Hours, One Hundred Ke, and Fifty Ying Circuits](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/06-人體為什麼要和一天對時-十二辰百刻與五十營/) aligns that smaller bodily cycle with the larger cycle of the day. Only after these baselines are in place can [Why Can the Pulse Be Fast or Slow, Long or Short? The Meaning of One Cun Five Fen per Movement](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/07-脈為什麼有快慢長短-一動一寸五分的意義/) meaningfully discuss deviations in speed and length, and [Tone, Breath, and Pulse Are Different Projections of the Same Process](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/08-音息脈其實是同一件事的不同投影/) close the first stage by proposing that tone, breath, and pulse may be different observational projections of the same qi-transformational process.

Once measurement exists, encoding can begin. [Why Does Each Person Have an Inherited Tone?](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/09-人為什麼有自己的稟音/) introduces the idea that each person may possess a different baseline, so individual difference cannot automatically be treated as pathology. [Yong, Gong, Shang, Jue, Zhi, Yu, and Su: Seven Scales of Human Tone](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/10-甬宮商角徵羽穌-人體聲音的七層尺度/) establishes a first layer of tonal classification, and [Ten Degrees of One Tone: Zheng, Shao, Zhong, Kai, Pan, Zhi, Yu, Jia, Tai, Shang](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/11-一音十分-正少眾開判質虞加太上/) divides each tone more finely. [Ancient ‘Pitch’ Was More Than High and Low: What Do the Ten Degrees Measure?](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/12-古人的音高不是高低而已-十分度在量什麼/) cautions that these ten degrees cannot simply be reduced to modern acoustic pitch. [Where Do An, Fu, Gong, Ge, Zhi, Ling, Jie, Bian, Sui, and He Come From?](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/13-安孚躬彁執淩解變隨盉從哪裡來/) then examines the relationship codes that emerge when states meet, while [Why Must the Same ‘An’ Still Be Distinguished by Left and Right?](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/14-為什麼同一個安還要分左右/) separates state from left-right spatial position. By [From Ten Tones to Two Thousand One Hundred: Why Does the Mirror Classic Need Such a Large State Space?](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/15-從十音到兩千一百音-為什麼需要這麼大的狀態空間/), the point is clear: the text is not trying to place people into a few coarse categories, but to construct a high-resolution state space.

Yet state alone is not enough for diagnosis. We must also know where a process begins and where it goes. [What Exactly Are the Sixty Shou? They Are Not Sixty Pieces of Music](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/16-六十首到底是什麼-它不是六十首音樂/) combines tone and lü into coordinates for depth and transformation. [Why Do the Twelve Lü Enter the Human Body? Pairing the Six Channels with the Twelve Lü](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/17-十二律為什麼會進入人體-六經與十二律的配對/) brings the Twelve Lü into the Six Channels, while [The Six Channels Are Not Six Lines: Warp and Weft, Pivot and Sustaining, and the Four Dimensions](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/18-六經不是六條線-經緯樞持與四維/) reconstructs the Six Channels as a spatial relation rather than six isolated lines. [How Do the Twelve Lü Become the Seventy-Two She?](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/19-十二律如何變成七十二舍/) introduces the she as locations of qi interaction, and [Forward and Reverse Sixty Shou: Qi Transformation Is a Path, Not a Static Table](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/20-順六十首與逆六十首-氣化不是靜態表格/) adds directionality. [What Is Shou? Why Must Diagnosis First Establish a Starting State?](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/21-首到底是什麼-為什麼診斷一定要先立首/) and [Shou and She: From a State Code to Where Qi Goes](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/22-首與舍-從一個狀態碼推出氣往哪裡走/) then make the basic diagnostic skeleton explicit: shou establishes a starting state, she indicates where qi interaction lands, and diagnosis begins to acquire the three dimensions of **state, position, and direction**.

The Twelve Channels must likewise be understood as a network rather than twelve separate tubes. [Why Does the Right Hand Pair with the Left Foot in Walking? The Mirror Classic’s Cross-Channel View](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/23-走路為什麼是右手配左腳-鏡經的交叉經絡觀/) begins from the intuitive example of contralateral coordination in walking. [Streams, Valleys, Joints, and She: Where Does Qi Actually Meet?](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/24-溪谷節舍-氣到底在哪裡交換/) reframes streams, valleys, joints, and she as nodes and exchange surfaces. [Qian, Gui, Cang, Sheng, Dong, Zhang, Yu, Zhi, Zhuang: The Nine Qi as a Life Cycle](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/25-謙歸藏生動長育止壴-九氣是一條生命循環/) describes a life-cycle sequence of the Nine Qi, while [Zhuan, Sheng, Jian, Bing, Tong, He: Six Basic Relations When Qi Meets Qi](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/26-專勝兼並通和-氣與氣相遇的六種基本關係/) supplies six basic operators for relations between qi states. [One Person Has Three Layers of Tone: Endowment, Current Movement, and Shift](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/27-一個人同時有三層音-稟受由行與差移/) separates inherited baseline, current movement, and temporal shift into distinct layers; [What Does the Eight-She Table Really Record? How the Twelve Channels Pair Across Hand and Foot](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/28-八舍真正記錄的是什麼-十二經如何跨手足配對/) can then recombine these dimensions across hand-foot channel pairings. [Seventy-Two She and Seventy-Three She: State and Movement Differ by One Number](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/29-七十二舍與七十三舍-位置與運動只差一個數字/) distinguishes an established position from movement through that position, and [A Complete Qi-Transformation Chain through Qian-Gui-Huangzhong: Sanjiao, Moyuan, Chong, Ren, Du, and Qiao](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/30-從謙歸黃鐘看完整氣化鏈-三焦募原與沖任督蹺/) demonstrates how a complete qi-transformation chain can be read through Sanjiao, Moyuan, and the extraordinary vessels.

At this point the internal model of the body is in place, but the human being is not a closed system. [What Does “Connecting with Heaven” Mean? The Human Being Is Not a Closed System](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/31-什麼叫通天-人不是封閉系統/) reintroduces Heaven. [Heaven Has Six Qi, Earth Has Six Tastes, Humans Have Emotions: How Three Inputs Enter One System](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/32-天有六氣地有六味人有情志-三種輸入如何進入同一系統/) treats environmental qi, flavors, and emotions as different inputs into the same system. [Zhuan, Sheng, Jian, Bing, Tong, He: A Six-State Model Across Nature and the Human Body](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/33-專勝兼並通和-跨自然與人體的六態模型/) shows that the relational grammar learned inside the body can be extended to exchanges between nature and the human organism. [What Happens When Two Tastes Meet? Fifteen Pairwise Taste Transformations](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/34-兩種味相遇會發生什麼-十五種味化組合/) and [Emotions Also Have “Composite Effects”: Fifteen Transformations of the Five Intentions](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/35-情緒也有合成效應-十五種五志之化/) examine the combined effects of tastes and emotions, while [“Essence Feeds on Qi, Form Feeds on Taste”: How Form, Qi, and Essence Transform into One Another](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/36-精食氣形食味-形氣精如何互相轉換/) brings those inputs into the transformation among form, qi, and essence. [Wind, Thunder, Grain, Rain, River, Sea, Heaven, True Qi: How the Nine Qi Enter Zang-Fu Networks](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/37-風雷谷雨川海天真-九氣如何落到臟腑/) and [How Many “Seas” Does the Human Body Have? The Mirror Classic’s Whole-Body Exchange Network](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/38-人體有多少個海-鏡經的全身交換網路/) then depict the zang-fu and the body's many “seas” as a whole-body network of convergence, exchange, and transport.

Only now do we enter pulse observation. [Cun, Guan, and Chi Are Not Just Three Boxes: What Are the Twenty-Seven Hou?](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/39-寸關尺其實不只三格-什麼是二十七候/) increases observational resolution beyond a simple cun-guan-chi tripartition. [Zhou, Cha, Jin, Duan; Yi, Yue, Kun, Lian, Zhi… What Do the Twenty-Seven Names Mark?](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/40-周差進斷毅悅昆斂致-二十七個名字到底在標什麼/) argues that the twenty-seven names primarily mark observation positions rather than twenty-seven diseases. [How Do We Read “Qian-Gui-Zhou, Zhi-An-Huangzhong”? Decoding a Complete Mirror Classic State Code](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/41-怎樣讀懂一句謙歸周質安黃鐘/) demonstrates how Nine Qi, hou position, tonal degree, and lü can be decompressed from a highly compressed sentence. [One She Contains Three Hou, One Hou Relates to Eight She: Hou and She Are Not the Same Layer](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/42-一舍三候一候八舍-候和舍不是同一件事/) establishes a crucial distinction: **she is where qi interaction lands; hou is where we observe it.** [How Do the Fifty Circulations Pass Through the Twenty-Seven Hou? From Single Pulse Point to Time Series](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/43-五十營怎樣穿過二十七候-從單點脈診變成時間序列/) then turns static pulse taking into a time series moving through the twenty-seven hou.

Observation of difference is still not equivalent to diagnosis. A model of normality must come first. [What Is Qiheng? Abnormality Must Be Defined from a Model of Normality](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/44-什麼是奇恒-異常必須從正常模型中定義/) therefore becomes the threshold of the diagnostic section. [Cun Mouth and Pulse Mouth Are Not the Same Thing: Three Cun, Nine Fields, and Twenty-Seven Hou](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/46-寸口和脈口不是一回事-三寸九野與二十七候/) clarifies the observation space of cun mouth, pulse mouth, three cun, nine fields, and twenty-seven hou; [Why Measure Three Cun at the Qi Mouth? Cun, Guan, and Chi Represent Three Sources of Qi](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/45-氣口為什麼要量三寸-寸關尺是三種來源的氣/) considers the different sources of qi observed at cun, guan, and chi. [The Pulse Is Not a Line: Surging, Ridge-Rising, and Three, Six, Nine Fen](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/47-脈不是一條線-波湧隴起與三六九分/) examines pulse geometry through surging and ridge-like forms, and [Huangzhong at Sixty Fen and Ruibin at Fifty-Three: How Does the Mirror Classic Detect Deviation from Pulse-Law Baselines?](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/48-黃鐘六十分與蕤賓五十三分-怎樣從正常脈律發現偏移/) establishes a comparison between normal pulse-law values and deviation. Only after those preparations does [What Is “Kuidu”? The Mirror Classic’s Diagnostic Algorithm Begins Here](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/49-什麼叫揆度-鏡經真正的診斷算法從這裡開始/) become meaningful as a diagnostic algorithm, while [Proper Position and Response Position: Disease Is Not Only “Where,” but Where Qi Loses Its Relation](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/50-當位與應位-疾病是氣在哪裡失去關係/) pushes the idea further: disease is not only a question of where something is wrong, but where positions that should respond to one another have lost their proper relation.

The final structural articles expand the algorithm to the geometry of the whole body. [“Tunneling” Among the Five Zang: Why Can the Heart Communicate with the Gallbladder, Stomach, Sanjiao, Bladder, and Large Intestine?](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/51-五臟穿鑿-為什麼心可與多個腑相通/) breaks the assumption that zang and fu communicate only in fixed one-to-one pairs. [The Depths of the Six Channels Really Have Numbers: Six Fen, Five Fen, Four Fen…](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/52-六經的深淺真的有數字-六分五分四分/) places channel depth and time on a shared numerical scale. [How Are the Lengths of the Twelve Lü Calculated? Tonal Division × She Division](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/53-十二律的長度如何計算-音分度乘舍分度/) reconnects tone, lü, and position through length calculation, and [How Long Are the Thirty-Seven Channel Passages? The Mirror Classic’s Whole-Body Geometric Ledger](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/54-三十七條經隧有多長-鏡經的人體幾何總圖/) closes a book-wide loop by comparing the lengths of the thirty-seven channel passages with the earlier whole-body circulation.

The true endpoint, however, is individualization. [The Shaoyin-Type Person: Why Can Four Pulse Movements Still Be Normal?](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/55-少陰之人-為什麼四動也可以是正常/), [The Taiyin-Type Person: Three Out, One In, and a Nine-Fen Pulse](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/56-太陰之人-三出一入與九分之脈/), [The Jueyin-Type Person: Four Out, One In, and a Three-Fen Pulse](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/57-厥陰之人-四出一入與三分之脈/), [The Shaoyang-Type Person: Two In, One Out, and Slow Breathing](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/58-少陽之人-再入一出與徐息/), [The Yangming-Type Person: Three In, One Out, and Three-Fen Surging-Leaping](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/59-陽明之人-三入一出與三分湧躍/), and [The Taiyang-Type Person: Four In, One Out—and the Mirror Classic’s Final Lesson in Individualized Medicine](/en/mirror_classic-_of_bian_que/60-太陽之人-四入一出與鏡經的個體化醫學/) progressively revise the initial idea of a single “normal person.” Normality is not abandoned; rather, a shared measuring system is established first, and then different type-specific baselines are defined within that common system.

After all sixty articles, the goal is not sixty isolated facts. It is the ability to convert ancient qi-transformation terminology into observable variables; to distinguish state, position, direction, and relation; to infer possible sites of qi interaction and pathology from what appears at an observation position; and finally to understand why an individualized model of normality must coexist with common rules. At that point the *Mirror Classic* begins to appear not as an ancient book of strange characters and numbers, but as a coherent architecture of **measurement → encoding → localization → inference → diagnosis → individualization**.
