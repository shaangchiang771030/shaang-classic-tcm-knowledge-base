A core sequence of nine terms appears in the “Eight She”:

**Qian (謙), Gui (歸), Cang (藏), Sheng (生), Dong (動), Zhang (長), Yu (育), Zhi (止), Zhuang (壴).**

The text says:

> Heavenly qi returns to the Heart; earthly qi is stored in essence; wind qi gives rise within fluids; thunder qi moves in the Lung; grain qi grows in qi; rain qi nurtures within liquids; river qi stops in bone; sea qi is Zhuang in the sinews; true qi is Qian in blood. Gui, Cang, Sheng, Dong, Zhang, Yu, Zhi, and Zhuang are where qi resides and where disease lodges.

These words first name **different states in which qi resides**.

## The Nine Qi are not nine substances

Gui, Cang, Sheng, and Dong look more like verbs than names of substances. This itself warns against treating the Nine Qi as nine invisible materials.

They are better read as different phases of one living process:

- **Gui**: returning or converging;
- **Cang**: storing and holding within;
- **Sheng**: beginning to generate;
- **Dong**: initiating movement;
- **Zhang**: extending and growing;
- **Yu**: nourishing and fostering;
- **Zhi**: stopping, stabilizing, or consolidating;
- **Zhuang**: a special graph whose usage in this text links sea qi with sinews and generative purification;
- **Qian**: occupying a central receptive root position and functioning as an important basis of the whole cycle.

Zhuang and Qian in particular should not be freely modernized from their ordinary character meanings; their significance must be read from their usage in the text.

## Why do the Nine Qi “all begin with Qian”? 

The text specifically says:

> The Eight She and Nine Qi all begin with Qian. Chong, Ren, Du, and Qiao occupy the center as roots and correspond to Qian, thereby manifesting the pivot of qi transformation.

A later passage says, “Qian is Moyuan true qi and the manifestation of the Great Void,” linking Qian with Sanjiao, Moyuan, and Chong-Ren-Du-Qiao.

Qian therefore appears not merely as one ordinary station among nine, but as a **foundational root position or initial condition**.

## The Nine Qi form a dynamic grammar

If a She were labeled only “Taiyin” or “Huangzhong,” information about what is currently happening would still be missing. The Nine Qi supply this dynamic layer.

Pairs such as Qian-Gui, Gui-Cang, Cang-Sheng, and Sheng-Dong occur repeatedly later. The active qi state is therefore often not one isolated term but **a transition from one phase toward the next**.

This is why the Eight-She tables are filled with two-character sequences: they record phase transition.

## The Nine Qi anticipate the “Tongtian” chapters

Later, “Tongtian” explains in more detail how wind, thunder, grain, rain, river, sea, and true qi connect with Liver, Heart, Spleen, Kidney, Brain, Stomach, and Sanjiao.

The Nine Qi are therefore not an isolated table in the Eight She; they form a skeleton for the later model of zang-fu qi transformation.
